The stem split's loading bar is the spectrogram

You asked for the loading screen of every stem split to be the spectrograms of the stems being split, appearing live. It can be live and honest: HTDemucs separates a song in chunks of 7.8 s, one after another from the start of the song, and each finished chunk already holds every stem's audio for that stretch. A short spectrogram of that stretch costs about 0.1 % of the chunk's time, so each lane fills to the true position in the song the moment the model gets there. Everything below runs on real stems: a 40 s clip of Fractals (bars 1 to 24: the sung intro over the pad, then kick and bass come in at 0:26) rendered by the app and split by the app's own code, with the chunks arriving at the times measured here.

Two details

They apply to every layout above, so you can see them live.
Why there is a draft stretch: chunks overlap by a quarter (1.95 s). When a chunk finishes, its last 1.95 s will still be cross-faded with the next chunk, so those columns are a draft that changes a little. P1 draws them at half strength and redraws them when the next chunk lands; P2 waits. Either way the amber edge sits at the last final sample.
Q

The quick split

The quick DSP split (stemSplit) also works through the song in order, in blocks of 262 144 samples (5.9 s at 44.1 kHz), and each block's four stems are final as soon as it is done (it keeps a 0.23 s margin on both sides). So it uses the same lanes, with no draft stretch, as one fast sweep: on this machine the 40 s clip took 3.2 s, about 0.08 times the clip's length, so a 4 minute song takes about 19 s here and a few seconds on a recent Mac. Choose Quick split above to watch it.

  • Under 1 s (a short clip): no loader at all, the stems simply appear (lane F's rule from NN/g: nothing under 1 s).
  • Today it runs on the page's main thread and pauses the app for about 0.45 s per block. The build should move it into the same worker as the spectrogram columns, or at least yield more often.
  • The quick split has no model to download or load, so the phases before the first chunk are skipped.

Measured in the repo

How: tools/design-lab/sg1/run.py opens the app (v0.294) through the AudioLab bridge, renders beats 0 to 96 of Fractals with renderMix and runs the app's own stemSplit on it with a timer on its progress callback. The neural split in headless Chromium crashed the page while the model loaded (the same 1.2 GB the app needs), so chunks_node.mjs runs the app's worker loop, unchanged (7.8 s chunks, 25 % overlap, linear cross-fades), in Node with the same engine: onnxruntime-web 1.20.1 in WebAssembly, the same model files from Hugging Face, the optimiser off as in the app. The stems on this page are that output. This container has 2 cores; your Mac will be faster, and the app runs with up to 8 threads there when the window is cross-origin isolated (the desktop shell is).
R

Research

How HTDemucs works through a song

  • Hybrid Transformer Demucs (Rouard, Massa and Défossez, Meta AI, 2022) is trained on 7.8 s segments of 44.1 kHz stereo; at inference the song is cut into chunks of the training length "with an overlap of 25% and a linear transition from one chunk to the next". The models only support segments up to 7.8 s.
  • Demucs's own apply_model walks the chunks from the start of the song (range(0, length, stride)), weights each with a triangle (power 1, which is linear at 25 % overlap) and divides by the summed weights. The app's worker does the same, so a stretch is final once the next chunk has been added.
  • The shift trick (--shifts) repeats the whole pass with random offsets up to 0.5 s and averages, "SHIFTS times slower" for at most 0.2 dB SDR. The app uses no shifts, which keeps one pass and a clean left-to-right fill. If shifts were ever added, the lanes would fill once per pass, or fill once and brighten.
  • Speed: the paper gives a real-time factor of 1.49 on one CPU core for the 7.8 s model (PyTorch). In WebAssembly here: 24.1 s per chunk on one thread, 13.1 s on two (one chunk moves the edge 5.85 s), so 2.2 to 4.1 times the song's length.

How other apps show it

  • Logic Pro Stem Splitter: no progress picture is described; the stems appear as a summing stack when done (Sound On Sound: "typically half a minute for an average-length song" on Apple silicon).
  • Ableton Live 12.3: a status with a task count, "1/2", for decoding and the passes (High Speed is one pass, High Quality several). No picture of the result while it works.
  • Serato and Engine DJ: a status message or a per-track icon that turns white when ready; Engine users ask for any progress at all ("no progress is shown when you click the job queue").
  • iZotope RX Music Rebalance, FL Studio, UVR, Moises, LALAL.AI: a bar or a job status; online services queue the file and return the stems. None we found draws the stems while they are made.
  • So the spectrogram as the loading bar is new. It also answers the two complaints: you see that it is moving, and you hear (see) early whether the split is any good before it ends.

Keeping the drawing cheap

  • The worker computes the columns, not the page: when a chunk lands it takes the final stretch of each stem, one 4096-point FFT per column (one column per lane pixel: a 4 minute song in a 480 px lane at 2x is about 23 columns per chunk per stem), folds it into 96 log-frequency rows and posts a small array (about 13 KB for six stems). Measured: 0.1 ms per FFT, so about 30 ms per chunk against a 13 s chunk.
  • One canvas per lane. Each chunk paints only its new columns (one putImageData with a dirty rectangle) and, for P1, repaints the draft stretch. Nothing runs per frame: the amber edge moves by a CSS transition, the chunk being worked on is a CSS gradient (F1) or a still hatch (F2), and the read-out text changes once a second.
  • Reduce motion (the system setting, and the app's Motion setting below Ambient): no moving gradient, no transition on the edge; the lanes still fill, because the fill is the information. Light mode (low power): the same, plus no draft stretch.
  • The mix's own spectrogram above the lanes needs no model, so it is drawn at once, before the download even starts.
  • The remaining time is the mean of the last three chunk times times the chunks left; before the first chunk it uses the speed this computer had last time (saved per model), and on a first ever split it says "estimating" until one chunk is done.
B

Who builds it

  • The splitter (stemSplit, NEUW, neuSeparate, stemAsk, stemsFor) predates the lanes and no lane owns it. The build task (SG2, prefix sg) takes it: the worker posts {t:'cols', i, s0, s1, fin, prov, cols} after each chunk, neuSeparate and stemSplit pass it on through a fifth argument, and SG_HOOKS.onChunk(job, msg) lets others listen.
  • Lane F (notifications, dialogs): the small card S1 inside the N1 stack, and the Split sheet's frame. SG2 adds SG_HOOKS.card(job) returning the strips; lane F puts it in the card when OV5 builds notify (until then SG2 draws it under mdJob).
  • Lane G (MD1): mdJob keeps the pill; for S2 it gets an optional strip through SG_HOOKS.pill(key).
  • Audio to MIDI (openA2M's stems step): SG2 replaces a2dbar with the lanes through the same module.
  • Colours: the stem colours of NEUM and the quick split (unchanged), viridis from INS (lane E, read only), the amber edge and units from the LOOK tokens (--unit, L2), W1 hairlines, motion tokens 0 / 50 / 80 / 120 ms (the edge moves in 120 ms).
