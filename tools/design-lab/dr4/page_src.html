The rules every instrument follows

  • Kept as they are (your standing picks): the mixer's Flat bars design, the half pan dial, the mixer header, the stereo field displays and the cables. For these only the drawing and the scales improve; nothing replaces them.
  • Units always shown (dBFS, dBTP, LUFS, LU, Hz), one decimal for levels, a real minus sign, frequencies with their note (X8). Numbers that are still settling show it (X15).
  • Ticks follow resolution: a mark per dB where the scale is wide, every 3 or 6 dB where it is crowded. Log frequency axes: long marks at decades, shorter at 2 and 5, labels only where they fit.
  • Colour means something: today's meter zones and colours stay; heat pictures use perceptually even maps (magma by default), always with a colour bar. Red never carries a meaning alone; a position or a mark goes with it.
  • Snappy and cheap: drawing at most at screen rate, nothing while hidden or off screen, half rate in Light mode with no afterglow. Each card shows how long its picture takes to draw on your computer.
