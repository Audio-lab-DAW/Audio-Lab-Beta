Speed and feel

Eighteen ways to make AudioLab quicker to work in and smoother to touch, for big psytrance projects. Each option has a mock-up you can try here, a measured or estimated saving, and the lane that would build it. Tap Pick on the ones you want, then copy the line in the bar at the bottom and paste it into the chat.

★ to ★★★★★ is the value for your way of working. The six with five stars are the recommendation (button in the bar). Already picked elsewhere and built on here, not repeated: the command palette K1 and menus R2 (task OV), design round 2 with the catalogue browser B3 (task DS), the commands registry and macros (task XT), the controller layer and Push 1 (task FT). Mock-ups are drawn at half AudioLab's size: 112 px headers, 30 px lanes, 22 px ruler.

Today's measurements

real app v0.263, Fractals (31 tracks, 364 clips), real browser input

Inputs were sent as real trackpad, wheel, pointer and key events (Chrome DevTools input), the same path as on your Mac, and timed from the event's own time stamp to the app's first listener (queue), to the end of the first arrangement drawing that shows the change (first drawn), and to 90 % of the final change (90 % drawn). The cloud machine has no graphics card, so the user-size column is slow everywhere; the small column (1280 × 800 at 1×) is closer to the Mac's speed. Three things stand out. A pinch costs about five times a scroll, and 15 ms of every pinch frame is page layout: the linked zoom also changes the track height, so the header rows are laid out again on every frame (S13). Every zoom is eased by 40 ms on top of the trackpad's own motion, which puts the view about 90 ms behind your fingers; the threshold where a lag in indirect dragging becomes noticeable is about 55 ms (Deber et al. 2015) (S9). A key played while the mixer draws waits for that frame, so timing jitter grows with how busy the screen is (S15). Script: tools/design-lab/ux1/measure_ux1.py.

Time verbs: duplicate, insert and delete time across all tracks

S2

Loop a clip by dragging its corner

A clip gets a loop length. Drag the small notch at the top right corner to repeat its content; repeats are drawn with a faint seam at each loop. Dragging the edge as today still trims or extends. Today a clip cannot loop, so a 1-bar bass becomes 16 copies.

S3

Pop-up browser with search, where you click

The browser opens at the spot you clicked (the empty lane, the device chain's +, a mixer slot) with the cursor already in a search field. Type a few letters: instruments, presets and effects in one list, fuzzy matched. ↑ ↓ audition while you move, Enter puts it on the track you clicked, Esc closes. What you choose for a word comes first next time.

    B · Keyboard

    S7–S8
    S7

    A single-key layer on the arrangement, and "G then" jumps

    When the arrangement has focus and the keyboard piano is off, plain keys do the common jobs: S solo, M mute (all selected tracks), Q quantize, R rename, L loop selection, [ ] previous / next section, ← → move by the ruler's grid, Enter play from the selection. K turns the keyboard piano on and off (then the letters play notes, as today). Two-key jumps as in Linear: G M mixer, G P piano roll, G B browser, G D drop.

    Click a header or a clip, then type here
    Saves
    A menu command (right-click, find, click ≈ 2.6 s and a decision) becomes one key (0.3 s). Today quant, solo, mute, rename, browser and pianoroll have no key; there is no next-section key; ← → move by a step that follows the zoom.
    Builds on
    XT commands registry (every key is a rebindable command), K1 palette shows the keys, the existing SCA list.
    Lane
    H (XT) for the key layer, C for the few new actions.
    S8

    Repeat the last edit with . and type a count first

    From Vim: . repeats the last edit at the new selection or playhead; a number typed before a command repeats it (15 ⌘D makes 15 copies). Each repeat is one undo step. Piano roll duplicate also moves by whole grid steps, so off-beat patterns stop drifting.

    Click here, then try ⌘D, ., 15⌘D (Ctrl on Windows)
    Saves
    Rolling bass for 4 bars 12 → 7; any repeated edit, one key per repeat.
    Builds on
    XT macros (a repeat is a one-step macro with one undo point).
    Lane
    H (XT).

    S9

    Direct trackpad: pinch and scroll follow the fingers, same frame

    A trackpad already glides (macOS adds momentum). AudioLab eases every zoom again by 40 ms, so the view trails behind. With S9 pinch and two-finger scroll apply 1:1 in the frame they arrive; only a notched mouse wheel is eased, and shorter (15 ms). Pinch on a trackpad or ⌘/Ctrl + wheel over either view; both get the same input. The graph shows the zoom you asked for (white) and what each view draws.

    Grab and fling with a mouse

    Middle-button drag (or the overview) pans both ways; let go while moving and the view glides on, slowing the way iOS lists do (0.998 per millisecond), and stretches softly at the song's start instead of stopping hard. For mouse and Magic Mouse users; trackpads keep macOS's own momentum. In the mock-up any drag pans.

    S13

    Zoom without page layout

    While a pinch is moving, the header rows are moved with transforms (no layout), and laid out once when the fingers rest. The arrangement picture is scaled between real drawings. Measured: today 15 ms of every pinch frame is layout and a pinch costs five times a scroll. This test runs in your browser: 64 header rows, a 2-second pinch, both ways.

    Tiled arrangement: draw only the new strip

    The arrangement is drawn into 256-pixel tiles that are kept; scrolling moves the tiles and draws only the strip that comes into view, and an edit redraws only the tiles it touches.     E · Response and animation timing

    S15–S18
    S15

    Steady timing when you play

    A note starts at the key's (or MIDI message's) own time stamp plus a small fixed delay, not whenever the busy screen lets the handler run, so there is no jitter. While a track is armed, meters and the mixer draw at half rate and no task runs longer than 2 ms. Musicians feel about 10 ms of delay and need jitter under 1 ms (Wessel & Wright). Press A S D F or tap the pads with the busy screen on.

    Saves
    Today a key played with the mixer open waited median 17 ms, worst 51 ms (cloud, small) and median 94, worst 188 ms (cloud, your size). With S15: one constant delay, jitter below 1 ms.
    Builds on
    The controller layer (FT-C1a) and noteOn; Auto buffer (256 samples) and the render size hint stay.
    Lane
    C (FT) for the timing, E (IN) for the meter rate while armed.
    S16

    Four motion timings, always interruptible

    One set of durations for the whole app, added to LOOK as tokens: 0 ms for anything you drag or pinch, 90 ms for hover and press feedback, 150 ms for opening and closing panels and menus, 220 ms for large moves. One ease-out curve. No animation on actions you repeat often; a click during an animation reverses it at once. Click quickly to feel the difference.
