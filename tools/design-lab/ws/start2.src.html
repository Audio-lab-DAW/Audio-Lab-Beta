The dipole's particles

Same field lines, same ball, four ways for the yellow dots to move.
Which planet's direction
The splash's dipole is tilted 10 degrees as at Jupiter, whose field points the other way to Earth's.
Why today's dots go back and forth. They copy charged particles trapped in a planet's radiation belts: each one slides along its field line and turns back at a mirror point, where the field is strong enough to reflect it, so it bounces between two points for ever (and drifts round the planet, out of the page). That is real physics, but in a flat drawing with no spiral it reads as a dot sliding to and fro. F3 shows that motion done properly.
What you described is the field's direction. A field line has a direction: it leaves the magnet at one pole, follows the line, comes back in at the other pole, and closes through the inside of the magnet. Dots moving that way (F1, F2) show the field itself rather than a particle, like iron filings that flow. Inside a uniformly magnetised ball the field is straight and even, so each loop closes as a straight chord, which F1 shows faintly through the glass.
