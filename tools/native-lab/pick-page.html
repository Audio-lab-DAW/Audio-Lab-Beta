AudioLab as a truly native Mac app

You chose a native Mac app over a finished DMG of the web app, with VST3 plug-ins now and Audio Units, CLAP and VST2 later. This page shows what was tested, the ways to get there with what each gives and costs, the one I recommend, and the questions only you can answer. The full plan with sources is docs/native-mac-plan.md in the repository.

Already proved

Three tests, built and run on an Apple Silicon build machine

The build machine is a virtual M1 with macOS 15, not your M4 and not your DAC. What it can show is that the code is right. What it cannot show is on your Mac, which is what the test app below is for.

TEST A · SOUND AND CORE AUDIOOne AudioLab effect, native, against the web engine 144,000 of 144,000 samples the same The Bitcrusher was ported from the worklet to C++ and compared bit for bit with the app's own render: three settings, also with dither and jitter. The same program listed the audio devices, opened the output on Core Audio, set a 64-frame buffer, switched the device to 96 kHz and back, and took exclusive use and gave it back. The web engine can do none of those four.
TEST B · VST3 HOSTA command line host on Steinberg's SDK 6 of 6 plug-ins loaded and played Surge XT (instrument), Surge XT Effects, and four of Steinberg's examples: parameters listed, notes played, audio run through, state saved and restored into a new copy, export mode, a switch to 96 kHz and 32 frames while running. Every bundle is scanned in a separate process; one was made to crash on purpose and the host stayed up.
TEST C · THE TEST APP"AudioLab VST3 test" for your Mac Runs, opens Surge XT's window A small native window app: finds your VST3 plug-ins, loads one, opens its own window, plays a pattern or your keys through the output you choose. On the build machine it loaded Surge XT, opened its window at 913 × 569 and played with 0 failed blocks.

Try it on your Mac

This is the most useful thing you can do now. It shows which of your plug-ins load, how your Gryphon and your AMS-24 look to a native program, and what a plug-in costs per block on the M4.

Download the test app

The file is AudioLab-VST3-test-mac-arm64.zip. It is not the AudioLab app and it sends nothing anywhere.

Opening an app without an Apple signature

  1. Double-click the zip, then the app. macOS says it cannot check it. Click Done.
  2. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll to Security. Click Open Anyway next to the app's name, then Open Anyway again, and enter your password.
  3. Pick your output at the top, click a plug-in, Load, Open its window, Play test pattern. Try the Buffer menu at 64 and 32.
  4. Click Copy report and paste it into the chat.

Said plainly, because you chose "truly native": after the engine has moved, the sound, the devices and the plug-ins are native, and the window is still drawn by the browser inside the app. Only the last stage removes that. It is the largest stage and the one a cloud task can check least, because it cannot see or feel a screen.

The recommended order

Five stages, each one works on your Mac

Bars are drawn to scale in tasks. The solid part is the low estimate, the faint part reaches the high one.

STAGE 1 · 8 TO 12 TASKS

Native output

A native engine opens your DAC itself. You pick the device, its sample rate, its buffer, exclusive use and its real format from the app. The web engine still makes the sound and hands it over.

Limit: latency does not drop yet. Playback gets better, live playing not yet.

STAGE 2 · 15 TO 25 TASKS

VST3 on a plug-in track

A track whose instrument is a VST3, with VST3 effects on it and on the master. The plug-in's own window, a strip in the device chain, its state saved in the project, export. Low latency from day one, since its sound never passes through the browser.

Limit: a VST3 effect on a track the web engine still makes has to wait for stage 3.

STAGE 3 · 70 TO 110 TASKS

The engine moves

Devices and instruments are ported one by one, each compared with the web engine sample by sample. A track whose whole chain is native runs native. When the last one has moved the browser leaves the audio path: the whole song at 32 or 64 frames, on all cores.

On the way: Fractals plays wholly native as soon as its devices are done.

STAGE 4 · 150 TO 250 TASKS

The native interface

The screens are rebuilt in native views, window by window, until the browser is gone. The download shrinks from 154 MB today to a few tens and the app starts faster.

Every window needs your eyes: a cloud task can take a screenshot, it cannot feel a drag at 240 Hz.

Will the sound change?

The honest answer, in three parts

  • AudioLab's own processors (19 devices, 10 instruments): the same bits are possible and test A shows it. The engine must be built without merged multiply-and-add instructions and must carry the same maths functions as the browser.
  • Devices built on the browser's own audio parts (16 devices, the mixer, both limiters): their arithmetic is Chromium's. It is open and can be rebuilt. Equaliser, gain, pan, delay and shaper should match; the compressor and the convolution reverb are measured and the difference written down per device.
  • Third-party plug-ins: no promise is possible. Test B measured it: two fresh copies of Surge XT playing the same notes differ by up to -7 dB sample against sample, because the synth starts its oscillators differently each time. Steinberg's delay and the mda synth came out the same every time. "Same project, same seed, same sound" will hold for AudioLab's own part of a song, and Freeze fixes a plug-in track when you need it fixed.
