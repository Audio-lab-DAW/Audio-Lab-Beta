AudioLab VST3 test 0.7 (tasks NM1, NB-N1, NL4, NL5 and NL7)

This is NOT the AudioLab app. It is a small native Mac program that tests one thing: can a native AudioLab
find, load and play the VST3 plug-ins on your Mac, through your DAC or interface, straight on Core Audio.
For Apple Silicon Macs (M1 and later), macOS 12 or later.

HOW TO OPEN IT (it is not signed with an Apple Developer account, so macOS asks once)
1. Download AudioLab-VST3-test-mac-arm64.zip and double-click it. You get "AudioLab VST3 test".
2. Double-click the app. macOS says it cannot check it. Click Done (not Move to Bin).
3. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security.
   There is a line about "AudioLab VST3 test". Click Open Anyway, then Open Anyway again, and enter your password.
   (The button is there for about an hour after step 2.)
4. From now on it opens normally.
If the button does not appear, open Terminal and paste this line, with the path of the app at the end:
   xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/path/to/AudioLab VST3 test.app"

NEW IN 0.7 (there is no 0.6 to test: 0.7 holds everything)
- Too loud: Phase Plant's own output reached +27.9 dBFS in report 7. When a plug-in's output passes 0 dBFS, the
  report gets a [warning] line (which plug-in, how loud, when) and the meter at the right of the middle row shows a
  red OVER mark until you click it. The app's own output never passes full scale: a safety limiter on the app's
  output only (the plug-in itself is not changed); the [level] line says how often it acted.
- Slow blocks: the [audio] lines now say when the 3 slowest blocks happened and what happened in the 100 ms
  before each one (a window opened or closed, a preset or program loaded, the plug-in restarted, a knob grabbed,
  the device switched), so a spike can be tied to its cause.
- Odd signs in plug-in texts ("17.1 ms" from Phase Plant came out with three odd letters) are gone: every text
  from a plug-in is cleaned like the app's own.
- Your DAC's bit depth: the [format] line says what the DAC gets on the wire. The Gryphon got only 16-bit integer.
  If a deeper format exists at the same rate, a [warning] says how to change it (Audio MIDI Setup > the device >
  Format), or use the new menu Device > Use the deepest format (only when you choose it; before and after are logged).
- A device that appears for a moment without a name (a Bluetooth reconnect) is no longer listed.
- [param] lines for values that changed in the plug-in's window although the plug-in never told the host.
- The report has no empty lines; "not played: play the pattern to test it" when no notes were sent; [load] gives
  the latency in samples and milliseconds.

TRY THIS FIRST
1. Set the Gryphon to 32-bit: in Audio MIDI Setup > iFi (by AMR) HD USB Audio > Format choose 32-bit Integer
   at 44.1 kHz, or choose the Gryphon in the app and use the menu Device > Use the deepest format.
2. Search "phase plant", Load, "Open its window", Play test pattern for ten seconds and turn a few knobs, stop it.
3. The same with Pigments.
4. Copy the report and paste it back.

NEW IN 0.5
- Silent instruments: Phase Plant and Pigments made no sound in 0.4 although the notes reached them. 0.5 sets
  plug-ins up the way JUCE-based hosts and Steinberg's own validator do (state given to the controller, messages
  between the plug-in's two halves from any thread). Both now sound.
- "Why silent?": loads the plug-in again in a separate process and plays it under several set-ups, then writes
  [probe] lines into the report. [diag] lines: what the plug-in asked for and what the app gave it.
- "Factory preset", [device] lines for devices plugged in later, Intel-only and broken plug-ins listed at the end,
  CLAP and Audio Units folders listed (not loaded), a plain-text report.

WHAT TO DO
1. The list shows every VST3 plug-in found in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 and in your own
   Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3. Lines that start with PROBLEM could not be read; the reason is next to them.
2. Pick your output at the top left (iFi Gryphon, Zoom AMS-24, ...). Try the Buffer menu: 64 or 32.
   New in 0.2, second row: Rate switches the device's sample rate from inside the app. "Exclusive use" takes
   the device for this app alone (other programs go silent on it until you untick it). "Device volume" is the
   device's own volume: move it here and watch it in Audio MIDI Setup, or turn it there and watch it here.
   Plugging a device in or out updates the list by itself.
3. Click a plug-in, then Load. Then "Open its window". Then "Play test pattern", or play the keys
   A W S E D F T G Y H U J K (click the list first, not the search field). Sound comes out at half level.
4. Try several plug-ins: an instrument, an effect (the pattern is then a plucked sound that runs through it),
   the ones you use most, and anything old.
5. Click "Copy report" and paste it back into the chat (or send the file
   AudioLab-VST3-test-report.txt from your Desktop).

WHAT THE REPORT HOLDS
Your Mac model and macOS version, every audio device with its sample rates, buffer range and whether it has a
volume the computer can set, every plug-in found or failed and why, what loaded, the time each plug-in takes
per block, and the buffer size. Nothing is sent anywhere; the file stays on your Desktop.

If you have no VST3 plug-ins yet: Surge XT is free (surge-synthesizer.github.io) and installs an instrument
and an effect.
