AudioLab sound quality: what to improve

You listen on a Gryphon and Stax, so small faults are audible to you. I measured every part of today's engine that can colour the sound: oscillators, distortion, filters, summing, sample-rate conversion, the limiter, export dither. Every number on this page comes from the app's own render (v0.263) or from a port of its code that gives the same number. Each option has a short A/B test at a safe level and a measured plot. Nothing here changes an existing project: every change that alters the sound is a new Clean setting, and old projects stay on Classic until you switch them. Full write-up: docs/research-engine-sound.md.

What is already right

Things that need no work

  • Summing: 64 tracks in Web Audio's 32-bit float differ from a 64-bit sum by −152 dBFS (rms), 136 dB under the mix. Nothing to gain from 64-bit summing.
  • The loudness meter's true peak is the exact BS.1770-5 filter (48 taps, 4x).
  • Psy Bass and Fractal already run at 2x with band-limited steps and ADAA. They are the model for the rest.
  • Sub is additive and band-limited; Bounce's modes stop below 0.45 of the rate.
  • Files you import are decoded by the browser's sinc resampler: images at −74 to −87 dB. That is fine.
  • Export dither is correct: TPDF ±1 LSB, and the first-order shaping works per channel.
  • The Soft Clipper at 4x keeps aliasing at −72 to −83 dB on a 2.5 to 5 kHz tone.
  • The Drive effect at low drive (30 %) is clean: −64 dB.
Listening setup

Before you press play

−12 dB

Start low. Every pair is matched in loudness, so the louder one never wins. The tests run in a 48 kHz audio context. If your output runs at another rate, the browser converts once at the end, the same way for A and B. A is today's algorithm, B is the improved one. A⇄B switches between them while the loop keeps playing.

Options

Eighteen options in six groups

For each option, choose Now, Later or No. ★ marks what this research recommends. In the plots, orange is today and green is the improved version. Spectra are peak-calibrated: a full-scale sine reads 0 dB. CPU figures are from this task's unoptimised JavaScript on a 2.1 GHz cloud core (one core = 20.8 µs per sample at 48 kHz). An M4 core is estimated two to three times faster.

Measured

All the numbers

"App" means the app's own render (v0.263, 48 kHz, through the AudioLab bridge). "Port" means today's algorithm copied from index.html into the lab, checked against the app. Aliasing is the power of everything that is not a harmonic of the note, against the harmonics, measured on 1 s with a Kaiser window (β 20). An ideal additive saw reads −165 dB. Scripts: tools/engine-lab/ae1/.
