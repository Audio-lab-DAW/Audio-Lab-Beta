What the research says

Musicians want AI as a helper, with control

In a 2026 survey of 1,200 musicians, 93% prefer more control even if slower, 43% want AI changes labelled and 39% want undo and compare (Muse Group). About 1,200 producers accept AI for technical jobs and resist it for creative ones (Sound On Sound, Sonarworks). Basis for I30, I31, I33.

Generated music must fit the session

Producers testing text-to-music tools hit wrong tempo and key, no way to edit, and no partial regeneration (Ronchini et al., 2025). Professionals distrust black-box mixing; amateurs want one button (Vanka et al., AES 2023). Basis for I32 and the ghost rule.

History you can play

Endlesss keeps every step of a jam as a playable rifff and lets you "go back in the timeline and keep working from there" (help page). Emacs undo-tree never loses a branch and can undo inside a region only (GNU ELPA). Basis for I22 to I26.

Seeing what plays

Strudel lights up the exact code that is sounding, by carrying source positions through every pattern (analysis, ICLC 2023). Masking meters exist only per plug-in pair (Neutron review). Basis for I1, I11, I12.

Accessibility is mostly an add-on

"The majority of commercially available DAWs are not designed to be compatible with screen readers" (Karpodini et al., BCU); blind producers lean on REAPER with OSARA. Basis for I34.

Low end is tuned by ear today

Kick-bass advice is to tune by ear, move bass notes by octaves and nudge the bass 1 to 5 ms (MusicRadar). No tool draws both on one pitch ruler with the key. Basis for I2.
