What the field is

It is Earth's magnetic field, drawn as a magnetic dipole: the field of a bar magnet in the Earth's core. Each line follows r = L sin²θ: it leaves the planet near one pole and comes back near the other, and L is how many Earth radii out it crosses the magnetic equator. So yes, the yellow centre is the Earth: this is the picture in every geophysics textbook.

The magnetic axis is tilted against the rotation axis, by about 9° today (9.2° in the 2025 field models; you will often read 11°, its older value). Close to the Earth the field really is a near-perfect dipole; the Van Allen belts sit on its lines at L ≈ 1.5 (inner) and L ≈ 4 to 6 (outer). Further out the solar wind reshapes it: squeezed to about 10 Earth radii on the day side (the magnetopause) and stretched into a tail hundreds of Earth radii long on the night side. The whole region is the magnetosphere.

The Moon is 0.27 times the Earth's size and 60 Earth radii away, on an orbit tilted about 5° to the Earth's orbit. It is far outside the dipole part, but around full Moon it passes through the magnetotail for about six days each month. In the icons the distances are shortened so it fits the tile; each card says what is to scale.
