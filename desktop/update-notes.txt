Clips look quieter: no coloured title bar any more. Each clip has a thin line in its track colour along the top, its name on the clip in a lighter tone of that colour, and a darker body, so notes and waveforms stand out. Clicking and dragging work as before; the Live, FL, Cubase, Logic and Bitwig looks keep their title bars.
Settings search got smarter: it knows other words for things ("latency" finds Buffer size, "sound card" finds Output device), shows where each result lives (for example Sound › Audio › Audio driver), and also finds options that live outside Settings, like the metronome, Capture, Snap or the mixer's Compact channels. Choose one and "Show me" closes Settings and lights it up where it is.
Settings has new pages: 13 short pages in four groups (Program, Working, Sound, Project) built from cards, with a strip of live numbers at the top (engine rate, buffer, CPU, frame rate, glass level and output; click one to open its page). Nothing was lost: every option moved whole to its new page, keeps its value, and the old page names still find it in the search.
New look details: a drafting grid of hairlines in the arrangement and piano roll, units in amber next to every value, yellow for everything selected, and denser tracks. Two new settings under Density and motion (Settings, Layout): Density (Dense, Today, Roomy) and Motion (Ambient, Signal and transitions, Signal only).
MIDI controllers, step 7: mixing surfaces. A Mackie Control or HUI surface (Behringer X-Touch, Icon, Mackie and others in that mode) now runs AudioLab: motor faders on 8 tracks and the master, knobs on pan or on the selected device, Mute, Solo, Rec and Select, banks, transport and the jog wheel, with track names and the song position on its displays. If yours is not found by its name, set it under Works as on the Controllers page.
MIDI controllers, step 6: keyboards and pad controllers. Plug in an Akai MPK mini, Arturia MiniLab 3 or Novation Launchkey and its knobs move the device you have selected, its pads play the drum kit, nothing to set up. Any other controller: open Controllers, pick a template and press Set up by moving, then touch each knob, fader, pad and button in turn. Save a setup as your own template; Play, Stop and Record on most keyboards work too.
New: the frequency map (command list: Frequency map). One picture of the whole song: time across, pitch from 20 Hz to 20 kHz up, every spot in the colour of the track that is loudest there, with the sections on top. Point at a spot to read it, click to jump there and select that track, or show one track or group glowing over the rest. It measures in the background the first time and remembers it.
Behind the scenes for the frequency map: AudioLab can now measure every track beat by beat (how loud it is in each band from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, in the middle and at the sides, loudness, brightness and its strongest notes), quietly in the background and remembered between sessions. The map that shows it comes next.
MIDI controllers, step 5: Session mode on your Ableton Push 1. Press Session: the pads show 8 tracks across and the song's sections down, lit where a track plays something; the buttons on the right (and the pads) launch a section: from a stop it plays from there, while playing it jumps at the next bar (or at the end of the section, or at once: the Launch setting on the Controllers page; Shift+button: at once). Press the waiting section again to cancel; Note goes back.
Glass for the transport and the device panel: both now float a little in from the window edges with rounded corners, so you see the arrangement around them; the bottom-left corner is properly frosted again, and the transport gets the lens, with a little extra bend on its top and bottom edges.
