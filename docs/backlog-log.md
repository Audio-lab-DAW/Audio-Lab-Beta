  - Each design is shown in both settings places as a small preview with its name.
  - The meters stay the existing `clip-path` bars with `<s>` peak lines, so instant levels, hold and the meter colour setting work in all 15.
  - Zones uses stepped colours from `lvStops()`. Level colour paints the whole bar in the colour of the current level (`mxsTick`).
- Fixes found while building:
  - The master's side meters never moved. `drawMixer` looks for `.fm` in the canvas's parent, and the master canvas sits in `.mxvw`. `mxsTick` now drives them.
  - With the picture hidden in the meter view, the side meters are driven too.
  - Channel names no longer shrink away in small windows (`.mx>.mxn{flex:none}`).
  - `mxsFit()` (wraps `mxFit`) applies the fixed fader height and makes faders shorter when the master would not fit. In a very small window it hides the master extras (`.mxtight`).

- iZotope Insight 2: one window made of modules (loudness, levels, sound field, spectrogram, spectrum, history) that are switched on and off with buttons in the title bar and share the space; the sound field puts the correlation bar beside or under the goniometer.

### Built (all new code sits before `askChoice`, names start with `anz` / `ANZ`; the old `spo*` functions are reassigned)
- **Steady spectrum scale** (`ANZD.spec`): the top of the picture is fixed at -24 dB (`ANZTOP`, measured so a very loud master sits about 12 dB under the top). Height can be 60, 72 (default) or 96 dB, or "Slow automatic", which only moves after the song has been louder or quieter for several seconds, in 6 dB steps. The old code rescaled on every frame.
- **Rising line when stopped: fixed.** Cause: silent bins (minus infinity) were turned into -120 dB, the treble tilt of 4.5 dB per octave was added, and the auto scale then zoomed in on that tilted line. Silent bins now stay on the floor and nothing is drawn without sound.
- **Glass border**: the window is now `.spov.anz` with a separate glass layer `.spgl` (class `lg` for the edge light, `rfAttach` for refraction) behind it. The canvases live in `.anzb`, a layer of their own above the glass, never inside it. Docked strips are solid (no glass at all).
- **Docking** (`anzSetDock`, `anzDock`, `anzSide`; button in the title bar or right-click): window, or a strip at the bottom (`#botOut`), top (`#topOut`), left or right (fixed, `#stage` gets a margin through `body.anz-l` / `body.anz-r` and `--anzl` / `--anzr`). Strips are resized by dragging their inner edge (`.anzrs`; `ov.dh`, `ov.dw`). One strip per side; `applyLayout` is wrapped so strips stay next to the main window. Docked analysers are saved and come back (`spoSave`, `UI.spov` now also holds `dock, mods, col, rng, dh, dw`).
- **Modules** (`ANZM`, drawn by `ANZD`, built by `anzBuild`): spectrum, spectrogram, stereo field, scrolling waveform, loudness (momentary, short-term and integrated bars, true peak max, range, target band), correlation (with a mark at the lowest recent value). Switch them with the buttons in the title bar, drag those buttons or the module labels to reorder (`anzWire`), or right-click for a menu (`anzMenu`). In a row the stereo field is square, loudness and correlation are narrow; in a column (left, right, or a tall window) they stack. The same modules work in the floating window. The first time an analyser is docked all modules are switched on.
- **Colours** (`anzCols` reads the schemes from `MXMODES`, so schemes added to the mixer views later appear here too): source colour, heat, three bands, bass heat, rainbow, fire, ice, purple spectral, AudioLab blue and teal, mono white. Uses `mxLut`, `mxC`, `mxPosRGB`, `mxBands`.
- **Idle**: after about 2 seconds without sound the pictures are only checked 3 times a second (`anzFrame`).
- Comparing several sources still works in the spectrum; the other modules show the first source. Loudness is always the master.

## 25 November 2026: v0.162, task 4 of 7 (cables in the mixer, FL Studio style)

### How routings are stored (nothing here was changed)
- Sends: `t.sends[returnId] = level` (0 to about 2, same value as the Sends bars, `SPEC_SEND`); applied by `busRoute()` and the offline render.
- Group output: `t.grp` = id of the group track (`grpOf`, `grpAnc`, `addToGroup`); `busRoute()` connects the track to the group input instead of the master.
- Sidechain: `f.p.src` (track id) on a Sidechain effect (`side`), and `m.p.src` on a Sidechain follower modulator (`t.mods`, type `side`, routes to parameters such as `t:<id>:vol`). Note: the Sidechain effect pumps in time with the song; its builder does not read `f.p.src` (only `scSrc` and `frzCut` do). The follower is the one that actually listens to another track (live, through `modFollow`).

### Built (all new code sits before `askChoice`, names start with `cab` / `CAB`)
- **Cable layer** (`cabDraw`, `cabList`, `cabGeo`): an SVG inside `.mxs` (so it scrolls with the strips) draws every existing send (solid line), group output (dashed) and sidechain (dotted) as a curve in the source track's colour, in a 46 px lane under the strips (`CABLANE`, class `mxcab` on `#mixWin`). Send cables get fainter at low levels; a switched-off follower is drawn faint. Several cables into one channel fan out. Legend at the bottom right (`cabLegend`).
- **Sockets**: a dot under every channel. Drag from it to another channel (or to the master): a dashed cable follows the pointer, the target outline turns green when something valid is possible, red when not, and the mixer scrolls when the pointer nears its edge (`cabTarget`, `cabMark`, `cabEnd`).
- **Drop menu** (`cabOpts`), only valid choices: "Send to" (target is a return, sends at 0.5), "Output to" (target is a group, uses `addToGroup`, no loops), "Output to Master" (dropped on the master while the channel is in a group), and "Sidechain into": switch an existing follower or Sidechain effect on the target to this source, add a new ducking follower (route to the target's volume at 50%), or add a Sidechain effect with this source.
- **Hover** a cable: both channels get an outline in the cable colour, other cables fade (`cabHi`, `cabUnhi`). **Click**: a small panel (`cabPop`) with the send level (the same control id as the strip's Sends bar, so both move together), a plain description, "Show <track>" and a remove button (`cabRemove`: stop sending, take out of the group (to the parent group or the master), remove the sidechain source, or remove the follower and put its targets back to their own values).
- **Toggle**: cable button in the mixer tab (`#mxCab`) and a "Cables" switch under Routing in the mixer settings drop-down and in Settings > Layout > Mixer (`UI.mxs.cab`, default on, `MXSD.cab`). All three stay in step through `mxsSet` / `mxsSync`.
- **Keeping it linked**: `changed()` is wrapped (`cabChanged`): if the tracks are different objects (undo, redo, track added, moved or removed) the mixer is rebuilt, otherwise the cables are redrawn when the routing signature (`cabSig`) changed. Track menu routing (`routeItems`), the Sends bars and the modulator source menu all go through `changed()`, so cables follow them. Every cable action calls `pushUndo()` first.
- `mxFit` is wrapped so the faders are sized for the room above the lane (it measures with the mixer temporarily that much shorter, plus 12 px spare because the volume numbers under the faders are often still empty when it runs; without that the pan row got squeezed onto the buttons).

### Tested (headless Playwright, 30-track project with groups and a reverb return, plus the Psytrance demo)

- Drag Kick to Plate reverb: menu offered Send plus sidechain choices; Send created `sends={..:0.5}` and a cable, one undo step; undo removed both. Lead to Master: "Output to Master" only. Snare to Kick: only sidechain choices. Removing the follower cable removed the modulator. Track menu "Send to" from `routeItems` made a cable appear.

## 25 November 2026: v0.163, task 5 of 7 (zoomed-out overview, Ableton style)

### Built (all new code sits before `askChoice`, names start with `ovw` / `OVW`)
- **Overview picture** (`ovwPaint`, `ovwGeo`): every track is a thin row (9 to 20 px, so 30 tracks fit; more than about 60 scroll) of plain blocks in the track colour, one per clip. No notes, waveforms or names. Muted tracks and muted clips are faint. A group shows a thin line over the span of its tracks. Bar numbers and the section labels stay on top, section starts run down as faint lines, the loop is a band on the ruler. Track headers become a 128 px column drawn on the canvas: a colour tab and the name (groups bold, members indented, selected track white). The background is darker than the normal view. The rows stop above the device panel and transport (`OVW.bot`, measured from `#fx` and `#tr`).
- **Getting in**: `zoomAround` is wrapped. When a zoom-out step is cut short by the view's limit, the cut-off part counts as "pushing against the end" (`OVW.acc`). After the end has been reached (180 ms earlier or more), a push of 0.25 (about half a wheel notch, a short pinch) or one more zoom key press opens the overview (`ovwEnter`). Also: Shift+O (new key command "Song overview on / off" in the shortcuts list, group View), right-click on the empty arrangement, the logo menu, and "Song Overview" in the desktop app's View menu (`desktop/main.js`).
- **Animation** (`ovwAnim`, `ovwFrame`, 170 ms, ease-out): the same picture is drawn with every position blended between the normal view and the overview, so rows compress and the background darkens. The last normal frame (`OVW.snap`) fades out over the first part, the DOM headers fade with CSS (`body.ovw #hdrcol`). Leaving plays it backwards onto a freshly drawn normal frame.
- **Inside** (events go to a see-through layer `#ovwL` over the canvas, so none of the normal clip handlers run and clips cannot be moved): pinch or Cmd/Ctrl-scroll zooms 1x to 2.2x around the pointer (`ovwZoom`), scroll or drag moves (`ovwPan`), a click puts the playhead on the nearest bar and selects the track, a click on a name only selects, Space plays. Zoom keys and map zoom act on the overview while it is open.
- **Getting out** (`ovwLeave`): zooming in past the top of the range (same push rule) goes back to the normal view with the beat and track under the pointer staying under the pointer. Double-click does the same. Esc, Shift+O, the "Overview" chip (`#ovwChip`, top left) and right-click > "Leave the overview" go back to exactly where you were. `V.ppb` and `V.th` are never touched while the overview is open and are written back on leaving.
- **Cheap drawing**: the picture is cached (`ARRC`); a playhead-only frame copies it and draws one line. While playing, full redraws are held to 10 per second (follow mode marks the view dirty every frame).
- `drawArr` is wrapped once more on top of the glass-copy wrapper; `ovwBase` is the wrapped original. `#hglass` is hidden in the overview so the half-resolution glass copy does not replace the left column.

## 25 November 2026: v0.169, Growl instrument and the AudioLab Pro logo (both asked by the user during run 8)

### Research (what makes a good growl)
Read: MusicRadar (FM growl in FM8, neurofunk bass and tearing reese in Serum), Sound On Sound "Dubstep secrets", ADSR (Skrillex growl in Serum), EDM.com "Beefy growls", Native Instruments (reese bass), Splice (talking wobble bass), two smaller blogs. What they agree on:
- **Source**: rich in harmonics. FM with ratio 1 or 2 and the amount moved by an LFO; a saw-like wave; for drum and bass two saws detuned by 15 to 30 cents (reese). Mono, with a clean sine sub underneath that is not distorted.
- **Mouth**: band-pass or vowel resonances that move in step with the beat (1/8 for classic wobble, 1/16 for rolling parts, 1/4 to 2 bars for slow neuro sweeps). Talking sounds jump between vowels far apart; neuro sounds use notch or narrow band sweeps over the whole note.
- **Dirt**: distortion before and after the filter, then strong levelling (OTT), then saturation or a clipper. Bit or sample-rate reduction for an edge.
- **Squeals and scrapes**: no page describes the "moving chair" itself. Built from the physics instead: a chair leg sticks and slips many times a second (rough bursts at about 20 to 60 per second, slightly irregular) while a narrow whistle rises in pitch.

### Built (new code before `askChoice`, names start with `grw` / `GW`; `grwInit();` sits right before the `/*VN-BEGIN*/` marker because the instrument registries only exist from there)
- **Growl** (`PRESETS.growl`, group Bass and acid in the instrument browser). Every note is worked out once as audio by `grwRender` and played as a buffer (`grwBuf`, `grwPlay`, cache `GWC`), so playback, freeze and export give the same sound and a repeated note costs nothing.
- Signal path: FM pair with a saw-like shape and an optional second detuned voice (Reese) → stick-slip bursts (Scrape) → drive → the mouth (three vowel resonances that move from Vowel A to Vowel B: OO, OH, AH, ER, EH, EE) → fold → crush → levelling (Squash) → the squeal (a whistle through a narrow band that glides from "Squeal from" to "Squeal to" over the note and jumps with every slip) → low cut, tone → clean mono sub added at the end.
- Movement: Speed in note lengths (1/1 to 1/32, with triplets), eight shapes (sine, triangle, ramps, square, Yoi, once over the note, random steps), starts again with every note. Stereo gives left and right slightly different mouths; the sub stays in the middle.
- 27 knobs in five groups (Source, Mouth, Movement, Scrape and squeal, Dirt and level), each with a plain-language tooltip (`GWTIP`). The picture shows the mouth as resonance peaks for vowel A and B and the moving one while a note plays.
- **24 presets** (`GWPRE`): dubstep (Yoi talker, Wub 1/8, Riddim grind, Screech, Robot vowels, Tearout machine gun, Deep growl, Metal gargle), EDM (Electro house growl, Complextro stab, Midtempo snarl, Bass house bark, Talk box lead), drum and bass (Neuro reese growl, **Moving chair**, Chair drag long and low, Chair screech short, Squeaky door, Foghorn, Neuro stab, Jump up squelch, Tearing reese 1/8, Rubber squeak), and Plain growl.
- **Logo 1** (chosen by the user): "PRO" in an outlined pill at the top right of the name (`.wmP`), in Settings and on the start screen. The small moving wave next to the old word is gone with it. The 15 options are kept in `docs/logo/audiolab-pro-logo-options.html`.

- `tools/engine-tests/growl.py`: all 24 presets rendered through the app at 174 BPM: no NaN, levels between 0.08 and 0.14 RMS, the sub share and brightness differ per preset as intended, two renders are bit-identical.
- Moving chair measured: the strongest band between 350 and 3000 Hz rises from about 600 Hz to 1350 Hz over the note and the loudness chatters at about 38 times a second.

### Built (new code before `askChoice`, new CSS before `.dacv`)

Zero effects = all off, solid panels (body class `fxzero` removes every backdrop-filter, `fxstill` stops CSS animations and transitions, the logo and bubbles stand still). The transport bar, track headers and device panel only have the arrangement behind them. A quarter-size copy of the arrangement canvas is blurred once (canvas `filter: blur()`, edges stretched outward first so the blur does not fade at the border) each time the arrangement is redrawn, and shown under those panels. They then have no backdrop-filter (body class `fxcopy`). The device panel (`#fxglass`) uses the picture in every level (body class `fxpic`), also in Full effects. So strength costs nothing and it is fixed at 75% of the old maximum. What does cost is the map: it is now made at 1/6 of the panel size on large panels (was 1/4; the divisor follows the width of the bent band, 2 to 6), about 2.25 times fewer bytes copied per frame per panel. `rfStyle` leaves the blur out of the filter list when the picture is behind the panel.

Each stop takes brightness and 38% of its colour from three points next to that edge in a 64 x 36 copy of the blurred picture: bright next to bright clips, dim over dark areas. Recalculated at most 11 times a second, only after the backdrop picture changed or while the pointer is in use, and written only when the text changed. The old glow (`filter: drop-shadow` on every rim, a full extra layer per panel) and the wide inner glows are gone; the base rim is about half as bright. Panels that are not over the arrangement keep a fixed rim. Strength slider kept; off in Zero effects.
- **Optimise for this computer** (`fxOptimise`, `fxFps`, `fxUndo`, `FXO`). ### Research on blur (part B)
- Live `backdrop-filter: blur()`: Chromium keeps the filtered result only while nothing under, inside or over the panel changes; any change re-filters the whole panel. Large radii are cheap per pixel because Skia blurs a scaled-down copy; the expensive band is just under 4 device pixels. Sources: grida.co/docs/wg/research/chromium/effect-optimizations, the project doc `claude/audiolab-liquid-glass-research.md` (Skia and Viz source links).
- Pre-blurred copies instead of blurring per frame: developer.chrome.com/blog/animated-blur (a blur that is not animated is rasterised once and reused).
- Dual Kawase / downsample-upsample: blog.frost.kiwi/dual-kawase (5 reads per pixel going down, 8 going up, cost grows slowly with radius). Needs WebGL or many small canvases; small 2D canvases may not be GPU canvases in Chromium, and drawing a GPU canvas into them forces a read-back. Not used.
- Chosen: a static pre-blurred quarter-size picture refreshed only when the arrangement changes. The blur itself is done by the canvas `blur()` filter (Skia, the same scaled-down method) on 1/16 of the pixels. Zero filter passes while only meters, the time display or device pictures move. The arrangement you see directly keeps full resolution and frame rate.

### Other things made lighter (part C)
1. Device panel: no backdrop-filter in any level (picture instead).
2. Rim glow: `filter: drop-shadow` removed from all glass panels.
3. Refraction map 1/6 size on large panels.
4. `#bLogo` glow: `steps(12)` timing, 6 changes a second instead of one on every frame; it sits in the transport glass, which was re-filtered for it all the time, also when idle.
5. `gbLayout` (reads sizes and styles of four elements) ran on every backdrop copy, up to 120 times a second while scrolling. Now at most 4 times a second on that path (`gbCopy(true)`); resize and the 0.7 s timer still run it directly.
6. With the picture, the half-size sharp copy `#arrBack` is not drawn (kept 2 x 2, it only passes clicks on).
7. Bubbles and loading pictures (`hxTick`) are painted once a second instead of 30 times when animations are off.
8. With the picture or Zero: the small blurs on device labels, the EQ overlay, the add-track buttons and tooltips are off.
Looked at and left: level meters at 120 a second in the headers (free with the picture, still re-filter the header glass in Full effects); `fxGlassSync` 0.5 s timer; visualisers and Fun views (they show sound).
