  - The meters stay the existing `clip-path` bars with `<s>` peak lines, so instant levels, hold and the meter colour setting work in all 15.
  - Zones uses stepped colours from `lvStops()`. Level colour paints the whole bar in the colour of the current level (`mxsTick`).

  - With the picture hidden in the meter view, the side meters are driven too.
  - Channel names no longer shrink away in small windows (`.mx>.mxn{flex:none}`).
  - `mxsFit()` (wraps `mxFit`) applies the fixed fader height and makes faders shorter when the master would not fit. In a very small window it hides the master extras (`.mxtight`).

### Built (all new code sits before `askChoice`, names start with `anz` / `ANZ`; the old `spo*` functions are reassigned)
- **Steady spectrum scale** (`ANZD.spec`): the top of the picture is fixed at -24 dB (`ANZTOP`, measured so a very loud master sits about 12 dB under the top). Height can be 60, 72 (default) or 96 dB, or "Slow automatic", which only moves after the song has been louder or quieter for several seconds, in 6 dB steps. The old code rescaled on every frame.
- **Rising line when stopped: fixed.** Cause: silent bins (minus infinity) were turned into -120 dB, the treble tilt of 4.5 dB per octave was added, and the auto scale then zoomed in on that tilted line. Silent bins now stay on the floor and nothing is drawn without sound.
- **Glass border**: the window is now `.spov.anz` with a separate glass layer `.spgl` (class `lg` for the edge light, `rfAttach` for refraction) behind it. The canvases live in `.anzb`, a layer of their own above the glass, never inside it. Docked strips are solid (no glass at all).
- **Docking** (`anzSetDock`, `anzDock`, `anzSide`; button in the title bar or right-click): window, or a strip at the bottom (`#botOut`), top (`#topOut`), left or right (fixed, `#stage` gets a margin through `body.anz-l` / `body.anz-r` and `--anzl` / `--anzr`). Strips are resized by dragging their inner edge (`.anzrs`; `ov.dh`, `ov.dw`). One strip per side; `applyLayout` is wrapped so strips stay next to the main window. Docked analysers are saved and come back (`spoSave`, `UI.spov` now also holds `dock, mods, col, rng, dh, dw`).
- **Modules** (`ANZM`, drawn by `ANZD`, built by `anzBuild`): spectrum, spectrogram, stereo field, scrolling waveform, loudness (momentary, short-term and integrated bars, true peak max, range, target band), correlation (with a mark at the lowest recent value). Switch them with the buttons in the title bar, drag those buttons or the module labels to reorder (`anzWire`), or right-click for a menu (`anzMenu`). In a row the stereo field is square, loudness and correlation are narrow; in a column (left, right, or a tall window) they stack. The same modules work in the floating window. The first time an analyser is docked all modules are switched on.
- **Colours** (`anzCols` reads the schemes from `MXMODES`, so schemes added to the mixer views later appear here too): source colour, heat, three bands, bass heat, rainbow, fire, ice, purple spectral, AudioLab blue and teal, mono white. Uses `mxLut`, `mxC`, `mxPosRGB`, `mxBands`.
- **Idle**: after about 2 seconds without sound the pictures are only checked 3 times a second (`anzFrame`).
- Comparing several sources still works in the spectrum; the other modules show the first source. Loudness is always the master.

## 25 November 2026: v0.162, task 4 of 7 (cables in the mixer, FL Studio style)

### How routings are stored (nothing here was changed)
- Sends: `t.sends[returnId] = level` (0 to about 2, same value as the Sends bars, `SPEC_SEND`); applied by `busRoute()` and the offline render.
- Group output: `t.grp` = id of the group track (`grpOf`, `grpAnc`, `addToGroup`); `busRoute()` connects the track to the group input instead of the master.
- Sidechain: `f.p.src` (track id) on a Sidechain effect (`side`), and `m.p.src` on a Sidechain follower modulator (`t.mods`, type `side`, routes to parameters such as `t:<id>:vol`). Note: the Sidechain effect pumps in time with the song; its builder does not read `f.p.src` (only `scSrc` and `frzCut` do). The follower is the one that actually listens to another track (live, through `modFollow`).

### Built (all new code sits before `askChoice`, names start with `cab` / `CAB`)
- **Cable layer** (`cabDraw`, `cabList`, `cabGeo`): an SVG inside `.mxs` (so it scrolls with the strips) draws every existing send (solid line), group output (dashed) and sidechain (dotted) as a curve in the source track's colour, in a 46 px lane under the strips (`CABLANE`, class `mxcab` on `#mixWin`). Send cables get fainter at low levels; a switched-off follower is drawn faint. Several cables into one channel fan out. Legend at the bottom right (`cabLegend`).
- **Sockets**: a dot under every channel. Drag from it to another channel (or to the master): a dashed cable follows the pointer, the target outline turns green when something valid is possible, red when not, and the mixer scrolls when the pointer nears its edge (`cabTarget`, `cabMark`, `cabEnd`).
- **Drop menu** (`cabOpts`), only valid choices: "Send to" (target is a return, sends at 0.5), "Output to" (target is a group, uses `addToGroup`, no loops), "Output to Master" (dropped on the master while the channel is in a group), and "Sidechain into": switch an existing follower or Sidechain effect on the target to this source, add a new ducking follower (route to the target's volume at 50%), or add a Sidechain effect with this source.
- **Hover** a cable: both channels get an outline in the cable colour, other cables fade (`cabHi`, `cabUnhi`). **Click**: a small panel (`cabPop`) with the send level (the same control id as the strip's Sends bar, so both move together), a plain description, "Show <track>" and a remove button (`cabRemove`: stop sending, take out of the group (to the parent group or the master), remove the sidechain source, or remove the follower and put its targets back to their own values).
- **Toggle**: cable button in the mixer tab (`#mxCab`) and a "Cables" switch under Routing in the mixer settings drop-down and in Settings > Layout > Mixer (`UI.mxs.cab`, default on, `MXSD.cab`). All three stay in step through `mxsSet` / `mxsSync`.
- **Keeping it linked**: `changed()` is wrapped (`cabChanged`): if the tracks are different objects (undo, redo, track added, moved or removed) the mixer is rebuilt, otherwise the cables are redrawn when the routing signature (`cabSig`) changed. Track menu routing (`routeItems`), the Sends bars and the modulator source menu all go through `changed()`, so cables follow them. Every cable action calls `pushUndo()` first.
- `mxFit` is wrapped so the faders are sized for the room above the lane (it measures with the mixer temporarily that much shorter, plus 12 px spare because the volume numbers under the faders are often still empty when it runs; without that the pan row got squeezed onto the buttons).

### Tested (headless Playwright, 30-track project with groups and a reverb return, plus the Psytrance demo)

## 25 November 2026: v0.163, task 5 of 7 (zoomed-out overview, Ableton style)

### Built (all new code sits before `askChoice`, names start with `ovw` / `OVW`)
- **Overview picture** (`ovwPaint`, `ovwGeo`): every track is a thin row (9 to 20 px, so 30 tracks fit; more than about 60 scroll) of plain blocks in the track colour, one per clip. No notes, waveforms or names. Muted tracks and muted clips are faint. A group shows a thin line over the span of its tracks. Bar numbers and the section labels stay on top, section starts run down as faint lines, the loop is a band on the ruler. Track headers become a 128 px column drawn on the canvas: a colour tab and the name (groups bold, members indented, selected track white). The background is darker than the normal view. The rows stop above the device panel and transport (`OVW.bot`, measured from `#fx` and `#tr`).
- **Getting in**: `zoomAround` is wrapped. When a zoom-out step is cut short by the view's limit, the cut-off part counts as "pushing against the end" (`OVW.acc`). After the end has been reached (180 ms earlier or more), a push of 0.25 (about half a wheel notch, a short pinch) or one more zoom key press opens the overview (`ovwEnter`). Also: Shift+O (new key command "Song overview on / off" in the shortcuts list, group View), right-click on the empty arrangement, the logo menu, and "Song Overview" in the desktop app's View menu (`desktop/main.js`).
- **Animation** (`ovwAnim`, `ovwFrame`, 170 ms, ease-out): the same picture is drawn with every position blended between the normal view and the overview, so rows compress and the background darkens. The last normal frame (`OVW.snap`) fades out over the first part, the DOM headers fade with CSS (`body.ovw #hdrcol`). Leaving plays it backwards onto a freshly drawn normal frame.
- **Inside** (events go to a see-through layer `#ovwL` over the canvas, so none of the normal clip handlers run and clips cannot be moved): pinch or Cmd/Ctrl-scroll zooms 1x to 2.2x around the pointer (`ovwZoom`), scroll or drag moves (`ovwPan`), a click puts the playhead on the nearest bar and selects the track, a click on a name only selects, Space plays. Zoom keys and map zoom act on the overview while it is open.
- **Getting out** (`ovwLeave`): zooming in past the top of the range (same push rule) goes back to the normal view with the beat and track under the pointer staying under the pointer. Double-click does the same. Esc, Shift+O, the "Overview" chip (`#ovwChip`, top left) and right-click > "Leave the overview" go back to exactly where you were. `V.ppb` and `V.th` are never touched while the overview is open and are written back on leaving.
- **Cheap drawing**: the picture is cached (`ARRC`); a playhead-only frame copies it and draws one line. While playing, full redraws are held to 10 per second (follow mode marks the view dirty every frame).
- `drawArr` is wrapped once more on top of the glass-copy wrapper; `ovwBase` is the wrapped original. `#hglass` is hidden in the overview so the half-resolution glass copy does not replace the left column.

## 25 November 2026: v0.169, Growl instrument and the AudioLab Pro logo (both asked by the user during run 8)

### Research (what makes a good growl)
Read: MusicRadar (FM growl in FM8, neurofunk bass and tearing reese in Serum), Sound On Sound "Dubstep secrets", ADSR (Skrillex growl in Serum), EDM.com "Beefy growls", Native Instruments (reese bass), Splice (talking wobble bass), two smaller blogs. What they agree on:
- **Source**: rich in harmonics. FM with ratio 1 or 2 and the amount moved by an LFO; a saw-like wave; for drum and bass two saws detuned by 15 to 30 cents (reese). Mono, with a clean sine sub underneath that is not distorted.
- **Mouth**: band-pass or vowel resonances that move in step with the beat (1/8 for classic wobble, 1/16 for rolling parts, 1/4 to 2 bars for slow neuro sweeps). Talking sounds jump between vowels far apart; neuro sounds use notch or narrow band sweeps over the whole note.
- **Dirt**: distortion before and after the filter, then strong levelling (OTT), then saturation or a clipper. Bit or sample-rate reduction for an edge.
- **Squeals and scrapes**: no page describes the "moving chair" itself. Built from the physics instead: a chair leg sticks and slips many times a second (rough bursts at about 20 to 60 per second, slightly irregular) while a narrow whistle rises in pitch.

### Built (new code before `askChoice`, names start with `grw` / `GW`; `grwInit();` sits right before the `/*VN-BEGIN*/` marker because the instrument registries only exist from there)
- **Growl** (`PRESETS.growl`, group Bass and acid in the instrument browser). Every note is worked out once as audio by `grwRender` and played as a buffer (`grwBuf`, `grwPlay`, cache `GWC`), so playback, freeze and export give the same sound and a repeated note costs nothing.
- Signal path: FM pair with a saw-like shape and an optional second detuned voice (Reese) → stick-slip bursts (Scrape) → drive → the mouth (three vowel resonances that move from Vowel A to Vowel B: OO, OH, AH, ER, EH, EE) → fold → crush → levelling (Squash) → the squeal (a whistle through a narrow band that glides from "Squeal from" to "Squeal to" over the note and jumps with every slip) → low cut, tone → clean mono sub added at the end.
- Movement: Speed in note lengths (1/1 to 1/32, with triplets), eight shapes (sine, triangle, ramps, square, Yoi, once over the note, random steps), starts again with every note. Stereo gives left and right slightly different mouths; the sub stays in the middle.
- 27 knobs in five groups (Source, Mouth, Movement, Scrape and squeal, Dirt and level), each with a plain-language tooltip (`GWTIP`). The picture shows the mouth as resonance peaks for vowel A and B and the moving one while a note plays.
- **24 presets** (`GWPRE`): dubstep (Yoi talker, Wub 1/8, Riddim grind, Screech, Robot vowels, Tearout machine gun, Deep growl, Metal gargle), EDM (Electro house growl, Complextro stab, Midtempo snarl, Bass house bark, Talk box lead), drum and bass (Neuro reese growl, **Moving chair**, Chair drag long and low, Chair screech short, Squeaky door, Foghorn, Neuro stab, Jump up squelch, Tearing reese 1/8, Rubber squeak), and Plain growl.
- **Logo 1** (chosen by the user): "PRO" in an outlined pill at the top right of the name (`.wmP`), in Settings and on the start screen. The small moving wave next to the old word is gone with it. The 15 options are kept in `docs/logo/audiolab-pro-logo-options.html`.

- `tools/engine-tests/growl.py`: all 24 presets rendered through the app at 174 BPM: no NaN, levels between 0.08 and 0.14 RMS, the sub share and brightness differ per preset as intended, two renders are bit-identical.

### Research on blur (part B)
- Live `backdrop-filter: blur()`: Chromium keeps the filtered result only while nothing under, inside or over the panel changes; any change re-filters the whole panel. Large radii are cheap per pixel because Skia blurs a scaled-down copy; the expensive band is just under 4 device pixels. Sources: grida.co/docs/wg/research/chromium/effect-optimizations, the project doc `claude/audiolab-liquid-glass-research.md` (Skia and Viz source links).
- Pre-blurred copies instead of blurring per frame: developer.chrome.com/blog/animated-blur (a blur that is not animated is rasterised once and reused).
- Dual Kawase / downsample-upsample: blog.frost.kiwi/dual-kawase (5 reads per pixel going down, 8 going up, cost grows slowly with radius). Needs WebGL or many small canvases; small 2D canvases may not be GPU canvases in Chromium, and drawing a GPU canvas into them forces a read-back. Not used.
- Chosen: a static pre-blurred quarter-size picture refreshed only when the arrangement changes. The blur itself is done by the canvas `blur()` filter (Skia, the same scaled-down method) on 1/16 of the pixels. Zero filter passes while only meters, the time display or device pictures move. The arrangement you see directly keeps full resolution and frame rate.

### Other things made lighter (part C)
1. Device panel: no backdrop-filter in any level (picture instead).
2. Rim glow: `filter: drop-shadow` removed from all glass panels.
3. Refraction map 1/6 size on large panels.
4. `#bLogo` glow: `steps(12)` timing, 6 changes a second instead of one on every frame; it sits in the transport glass, which was re-filtered for it all the time, also when idle.
5. `gbLayout` (reads sizes and styles of four elements) ran on every backdrop copy, up to 120 times a second while scrolling. Now at most 4 times a second on that path (`gbCopy(true)`); resize and the 0.7 s timer still run it directly.
6. With the picture, the half-size sharp copy `#arrBack` is not drawn (kept 2 x 2, it only passes clicks on).
7. Bubbles and loading pictures (`hxTick`) are painted once a second instead of 30 times when animations are off.
8. With the picture or Zero: the small blurs on device labels, the EQ overlay, the add-track buttons and tooltips are off.
Looked at and left: level meters at 120 a second in the headers (free with the picture, still re-filter the header glass in Full effects); `fxGlassSync` 0.5 s timer; visualisers and Fun views (they show sound).

## 25 November 2026: v0.174, run 13: particle devices, part 2 (Emit, a granular instrument)

### Research (what Ableton's Emit does)
Read: Top Music Arts "Sound Design with Inspired by Nature: Emit" (topmusicarts.com/blogs/news/sound-design-with-inspired-by-nature-emit), Attack Magazine "Getting freaky with Live 11's new Inspired by Nature devices", Ableton's pack page (ableton.com/en/packs/inspired-nature). The Live manual does not cover the pack; the pack's own lesson text was not reachable.
- Confirmed by those pages: the picture is a spectrogram (time left to right, pitch bottom to top). An emitter is a box you move and resize; particles are born at a random spot inside it and play the part of the sample they pass over. Speed does not change the pitch (Ableton uses a phase vocoder). Height drives a band filter, or the pan (Filter / Pan switch). Edges bounce or wrap. Angle, Width (range of angles), Bipolar (both directions; a particle moving left plays backwards), Rate, Lifetime, Burst (particles per shot), Spread (how far apart the burst is spaced over the interval), Grains (most particles), Force (start speed), Friction, Pitch in semitones, Blur (spectral smear), Filter width and Q, two emitters, two LFOs and envelopes routed to parameters, an LFO on Angle makes the emitter rotate, notes can set the pitch or only the rhythm.

### Research (what Ableton's Vector Delay does)
Read: Dillon Bastan's Vector Delay manual (dillonbastan.com/inspiredbynature_manuals/Vector%20Delay%20User%20Manual.pdf) and Top Music Arts "Sound Design with Inspired by Nature: Vector Grain & Vector Delay" (topmusicarts.com/blogs/news/sound-design-with-inspired-by-nature-vector-grain). Both were read through summaries, not word for word.
- Confirmed by those pages: every particle is one independent delay line. Voices (number of particles, also the most the emitter makes), Scale, Speed, Mix. Three pages: delay, forces, particles and emitter. Delay: Time in milliseconds or synced, Glide (0 = no glide with crossfading delay lines; above 0 the pitch bends while the time slides), Feedback, D (Damp: a little high and low cut in the feedback), Pitch in steps, F (fine), **Recycle = the pitch shift moves into the feedback so every repeat is shifted again** (the task text had guessed "re-launch a particle"; the manual and the article agree on this meaning), a scale for pitch that is driven by a particle, Pan, Gain, Reverse, filter types. Five knobs (X, Y, distance, speed, mass) each with a target menu under it; mass never changes after a particle is made. Forces: attractor, flow field, magnetism by charge. Particles: Charge (chance that a new particle is positive), Edge (bounce, clip, wrap), Mass, Rnd > Mass. Emitter (off by default): particles are made and die on synced intervals; Burst, Rate, Life, Skip, Rnd > Rate, Rnd > Life, Fade and Fade shape (left ramp down, middle triangle, right ramp up).
- **Guesses** (no page gives them): every range, unit and default; the list of targets (Time, Pitch, Pan, Gain, Filter) and how far each goes at 100% (time 2 octaves, pitch 24 semitones, cutoff 4 octaves, gain to zero or double); the 50 ms window of the pitch shifter; reverse in pieces as long as the delay time; how strong Damp is (cuts below 140 Hz and above 6.5 kHz, 6 dB per octave); emitter direction, spread and start speed. The physics knobs are Vector Grain's (see v0.175).
- Not built from Ableton: the X and Y offset sliders (here the two range bars of the other Vector devices), the Velocity limit, attractor position and "Attracts" charge choice (the pull goes to the middle of the range), drawing the flow field by mouse and its grid size, Edge modes Clip and Wrap, Save, Spawn area, On click (random, freeze), Rnd > Rate and Rnd > Life, the ladder filters, Res and LP-HP, Scala files.

### Built (new code before `askChoice`; names start with `vdl` / `VDL`; `vdlInit();` sits right after `vmInit();`)
- **Vector Delay** (`FXDEF.vdelay`, in the effect browser under Delay & space; registered with `ND` inside `vdlInit`, in the set `PBX`, `PBXW.vdelay`). Worklet `al-vdelay` (module string `VDLWL`, added to both module lists in `wlLoad`), made with `wk`; `x.sp = p => vdlSp(p, c)` builds the settings.
- `vdlCore(C)` sits on top of the shared field `vfCore()` (unchanged) and is used by the worklet and by the preview picture, so there is one copy: `sim(seed)`, `step(S,P)` (one step of 1/100 s: emitter on a beat grid, lifetime, fade, charge of new particles, then `C.step`), `snap` / `load` (save points; the field's random generator was replaced by one whose state can be saved), `calc(P,values,out)` (what the five values of a particle do: time factor, semitones, pan, gain, cutoff factor; pitch steps free, semitones or a scale).
- **Song time**: while the song runs the field is always the one that belongs to the song time: stepped from the start of the song, with a save point every 4 s. When the time jumps back (loop, new start) the engine goes back to a save point or to the start; a jump forward is caught up at 75 steps per 64 samples; an export does all of it at once. While the song is stopped the field runs freely. Turning a knob that moves the particles while the song plays drops the save points and carries on from where the particles are; the next start of playback works them out again from the start, as an export does.
- **Sound**: every particle has a delay line of its own (5.5 s of stereo memory, made when the particle first appears) with its own feedback, so the feedback of a line is never more than the knob says (the first version shared one memory and fed the sum of all lines back; with many lines close together that howled, the Metal combs preset sat at -6 dB for ever). Two reading heads per line fade into each other all the time (50 ms, or one delay time when reversed). A head takes over a new delay time and pitch only while it is silent: a jump in delay time is a short crossfade, a pitch shift is a sliding delay, reverse is a head running backwards. With Glide above 0 the delay time slides instead. Recycle off: the feedback is read a second time without the shift (four readings per line instead of two). A filter per line (off, low pass, band pass, high pass, notch). In the feedback: a DC blocker, Damp, and a soft limit. The level of the lines is divided by their number when they all have the same delay time and by its square root when they are spread in time. Mix: up to 50% the sound going in stays at full level.
- The engine works in pieces of 64 samples counted from the start, so the result does not depend on the block size of the audio system (128 live, 512 in an export).
- Controls, on five pages (buttons Delay, Vectors, Forces, Particles, Emitter; Voices, Scale, Speed and Mix are on every page; the page is stored as `p._td` and so on; a wrapper of `fxDevHtml` hands only the open page to the usual device code): Delay: Time (milliseconds or a note length), Glide, Feedback, Pitch, Fine, Pan, Gain, Filter, Q, filter type, Damp, Recycle, Reverse. Vectors: X, Y, Dist, Speed, Mass, each with a choice of what it drives, and Steps (free, semitones, a scale). Forces: Motion Forces / Flow, Gravity, Magnet, Center, Drag or Flow, Detail, Drift, Drag. Particles: Mass, Rnd > Mass, Charge, Start speed, Seed. Emitter: on, Every (note length), Burst, Life in beats, Skip, Fade, Fade shape, Angle, Spread. Tooltips in plain language on every knob and choice (set after `devLayout`).
- Picture (`vdlDraw`, `vdlGeo`, `vdlHnd`): the shared field painter `vfPaint` in orange, a faint line from the middle to every particle, size shows how loud a line is, colour its place between left (blue) and right (yellow). Handles: the ends of the two range bars, the emitter (drag to move) and a white dot to aim it. Live the picture shows the engine's own particles (30 times a second), otherwise a preview of the same steps.
- **14 presets**: Echo cloud (the default), Three in orbit, Wide slap, Still taps, River (flow, tape glide), Rising shimmer, Falling cascade, Fifths above (once), Scale chimes, Backwards swarm, Bursts every beat, Sprinkler, Metal combs, Thick chorus.
- **Fix found on the way (affects every effect that follows the song time in an export, also Tape stop and the modulated effects):** `originT` took the start beat of an offline render from the global `SIDE_B0`, which the next render overwrites. ### Research (the user asked for it)
- Bouncing ball delays in other plug-ins: Sinevibes Dispersion ("a single round of such delay bounces is always summing up to a precise tempo fraction", up to 32 delay lines, pan spread so the ball moves across the stereo field; pluginboutique.com, sinevibes.com/dispersion, kvraudio.com), Boscomac Bouncing Ball for Reaktor (number of bounces, time between bounces that can follow the tempo, gravity, decay; audiofanzine.com, rekkerd.org), G-Delay for Max for Live (a fixed number of taps instead of feedback, Gravity and Antigravity modes, the interval sets the largest gap; isotonikstudios.com), the Audacity Bouncing Ball Delay (decay per bounce, delay time, number of bounces, a version with pan; plugins.audacityteam.org), a "Panning gravity delay" MIDI patch that retriggers notes as if bouncing (patchstorage.com). Ableton's own is Bouncy Notes in Inspired by Nature (copied in v0.166). Search results and page summaries were read, not the manuals.
- How games do ball physics: a fixed time step with an accumulator so the result is the same on every machine, semi-implicit Euler or velocity Verlet as the step (Glenn Fiedler, "Fix Your Timestep!", gafferongames.com; summaries of it on gamedev.net and others).
- What was taken from it: (1) taps instead of feedback for the delay, so every repeat lands exactly where the formula puts it; (2) both ways of tying to the tempo: the first gap is the note length, or the whole round is the note length (Dispersion's way); (3) no time stepping at all for a single ball on a flat floor: the landing times are a closed formula, which is exact and costs nothing while playing. Time stepping is only used where forces change all the time (Orbit, the Vector devices), there with a fixed step of 64 samples.

### How the rhythm is real physics
A ball thrown up with speed v is in the air for 2v/g and comes back with speed v; a bounce multiplies the speed by the bounciness e. For a chosen note length T the gravity is solved so that a ball that rises to its height h comes down after exactly T: g = 8h/T². Then the gaps are T, Te, Te² and so on. Steady is e = 1 (it never loses height: a bounce on every step for as long as Lifetime lasts), Halving is e = 1/2 (1/4, 1/8, 1/16), Two thirds is e = 2/3, By friction takes e from the Friction and Mass knobs, Whole round makes the first gap T(1 - e) so that all gaps together are exactly T. In a rhythm the ball starts on the keys at the note (the note itself is the first landing), and the Gravity and Speed knobs no longer change the timing, only Free mode uses them. The picture shows the same flight.

### Built
- **Bouncy Notes** (`bnBalls`, `bnRun` changed in place; new `bnDeg`, `BNWALK`, `BNHARM`; `bn2Init();` after `orbInit();`):

  - 9 new presets in two groups: On the beat (Every beat for ever, Ratchet, Falling by two thirds, One round fills a bar, Steady 1/8 walk up the scale) and Melody and chords (Ratchet melody, Chords on every beat, Falling sevenths, Skipping fifths). The old presets are unchanged (Rhythm free).

Changing a setting fades the old taps out and the new ones in over 30 ms. The picture shows the flight, a dot per landing (size = level, colour = side) and a ball running along. 7 presets. Tooltips on everything.

### What the old "live mode" was, and what it is now
- Before: a click in the instrument browser made a temporary track (`ib.t`) with a fresh copy of the device, drew it as a real, editable device inside the tile (`ibLiveRender`, `IBCTX` makes `curT()` return that temporary track), and played a phrase through the preview bus. The track itself changed only on Use (`chooseIB`). The effect browser did the same with a temporary effect chain (`ibFxChain`).
- Now, in change mode (`ib.chg`, state in `CHG`): the temporary track holds **the very object that is on the track** (`ib.t.inst === track.inst`, `ib.t.fx[0]` is the effect in the chain). So the tile shows and edits the real device, and what is heard is the track. The preview chain is not built in change mode.

### How it is built (search "Change with live browsing" in index.html, before `askChoice`)
- `chgOpen(mode,t,f)`: called by the wrapped `openBrowser(track)` and by `duChangeFx`. Takes a copy of the device (`CHG.orig`) and one undo snapshot (`CHG.snap`).
- `chgSwap(k)`: called from the wrapped `renderIBTry` when the selection differs from what is on the track. Instrument: the instrument object is refilled in place. Effect: one entry of `t.fx` is replaced and `syncGraph()` builds the one missing node. Nothing else is rebuilt: the device strip behind the browser is not drawn during the visit (the one device in it is emptied once, `chgBlank`).
- `chgRevoice(t)`: the track gets a fresh input node; the old one, with everything connected to it (clip chains, voice engines, note tails), is faded out and dropped. The notes to play again come from a list kept by a wrapper of `synthB` (`CHGN`: every note the scheduler hands over while playing). Notes of instruments built from browser nodes continue as if they had played from their start (`pastShim`, as presets already did); instruments with a voice engine start the held notes again with their own attack.
- `chgEnd(keep)`: keep pushes the one snapshot and calls `changed(true)`; otherwise the copy is put back. A watcher on the browser's `hidden` attribute ends the visit however the browser was closed.
- Double-click: the second press is recognised by time and place (`pointerdown`, 500 ms, 8 px) because the first click redraws the tile and the browser's own double-click may never arrive. On a tile that was already open, a double-click on a control or a picture still belongs to the device.
- `chgNav(key)`, `chgSee(tile)`: arrow keys and bringing a tile into view (above the piano).
- Opening Change a second time reuses the tiles that are already built (wrapper of `renderPlaneInst`).
- Nothing in the sound code was changed. The only addition on the audio side is the note list and the fresh input node at a swap.

## 25 November 2026: v0.184, clean preset switch and picking up notes in the middle (task I, from the user's message)

The user's words: "we do have some problems with changing presets on synths. And I suspect we might have the same problem for effects. [...] The previous preset still keeps going under your next preset. [...] when you change presets, you instantly hear one or the other preset or the next without hearing the previous one. [...] Let's say you have like a long pad note and you're in the middle of it. It continues in the middle of the note. [...] Look into how other DAWs do this and what the best algorithms are to keep everything light and snappy".

### What changed for the user
- **A preset change is a clean switch.** When the preset of an instrument changes while the song plays (menu, arrow buttons, undo, a new sample, a whole new instrument through Change), the old sound is gone within 13 to 19 ms: the notes that were already handed to the audio engine ahead of time, held notes, release tails, and the echo or reverb of the old sound in the delays and reverbs of that track. The new preset sounds from that same moment.
- **Notes that are sounding are picked up.** A long pad that is in the middle of its note carries on with the new preset at once. A fast pattern keeps its timing: every note of the look-ahead is handed over again at its own time, none twice, none missing.
- **The same when Play is pressed in the middle of a long note**, after a jump of the playhead (a jump is a stop and a start) and at the start of every loop round: the note sounds. Before, it stayed silent until its next start.
- **Effects**: a preset change on Echo delay, Timeless delay, Reverb, Space reverb, Blur, Vector Delay, Bounce Delay or Resonator clears the memory of the effect; the old tail fades out in 8 ms. Other effects take the new settings as before, without a break. The Change browser's effect swap now fades the old effect out the same way (it was a hard switch in v0.183).
- **Keys held on the keyboard** carry on with the new preset and still end when they are let go.
- **The test note of a preset change is left out while the song plays** and the track's own notes were picked up (it used to play a C over the song).
- **Settings, Audio system, Playback**: "Pick up notes that are already sounding" (on by default). Switched off, a note is only heard when the playhead passes its start; the clean switch stays.

### Part 2: how others do it
- **Chasing notes** is a standard option. Logic Pro (Project settings, MIDI, Chase) sends a note-on for a note when playback starts between its start and its end, with extra switches for notes held by the sustain pedal and for drum and sample channels, where a sample started late would be out of step (read from Apple's page through a summary; the page says nothing about where a sample starts). Ableton Live has "Chase MIDI Notes" in the Options menu: starting in the middle of notes whose starts lie before the start point plays them (forum reports, some of them about cases where it failed; no official text read). Cubase has "Chase Events" in the MIDI preferences; its manual page could not be read in this run (the page is a stub that points to a 1.2 MB PDF), so nothing is claimed about it here.
- All of these send a plain note-on, so the instrument starts the note from its attack and a sample from its beginning. AudioLab knows its own instruments, so it can do better where it is cheap: start the envelopes where they would be by now, and samples at the right spot.
- **All notes off against all sound off** (MIDI controllers 123 and 120): as far as the MIDI standard is known to me, 123 lets go of the keys, so releases ring on, and 120 silences at once including releases and effects inside the instrument. This could not be confirmed from the two pages fetched for it (one returned a home page, the other only names 123). A preset switch that must not let the old sound ring needs the second kind, with a short fade so it does not click. That is what was built.
- **Program changes in plug-ins**: no source read; the common ways are a short crossfade between the old and the new state or a fast fade of all voices. The cheapest that is exact to the sample here is a gain ramp on what the track's notes play into.
- Sources: https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro/lgcp621f927c/mac , https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?p=1744482 , https://steinberg.help/cubase_ai/v13/en/cubase_nuendo/topics/preferences/preferences_midi_r.html (not readable), https://www.sweetwater.com/sweetcare/articles/what-controller-number-midi-all-notes-off-midi-panic/

### Part 3: how it is built (search "Clean switch and picking up notes" in index.html, before `askChoice`)
- **One rule for all instruments, in one place: `chsTrack(t)`.** It is called from one wrapper of `changed()` when the instrument's signature changed (preset key, user preset, instrument kind, sample; `chsSig`), and from `chgRevoice` (the Change browser, whose own re-voicing code and note list were removed).
  1. `chsWarm`: tables and samples the new sound needs are made first.
  2. `chsCut`: the track gets a fresh input node. The new path is wired before the old one is taken away (a disconnect followed by a connect left one block of silence, measured). The old input goes through a gain that fades to zero in 4 ms, with everything that hangs on it: notes of the look-ahead, held notes, tails, clip chains. Nothing has to be tracked per note.
  3. Voice engines (`al-poly`, `al-grain`, Emit, Vector Grain, Vector FM, Orbit): the engine that was playing stays on the old input and fades with it, exact to the sample; then it is emptied and kept as the track's spare, and the spare of last time takes over at once with its samples and tables still loaded. A message to an engine takes up to one output buffer to arrive, so telling the engine to stop was not exact enough (measured: 3 to 15 ms). The spare is made when Play is pressed, once per track (`chsSpare`); an engine with no voices does nothing in its loop. Venom cuts hard when told to stop and has effects inside, so it always gets a fresh engine, which is also kept ready.
  4. Effects with memory on that track are replaced by fresh nodes (`chsFxOut`); the old node fades out in 5 ms into the place it played into, and that fade starts before the fresh node is built (a Reverb takes about 40 ms to build).
  5. `chsResched`: the scheduler runs once more for this one track (`play.only`) from the moment of the cut to where it had got to (`play.sched`), with picking up switched on (`play.chs`). It also handles the 150 ms before a loop end, when the scheduler is already in the next round and the sound is not.
  6. The fade of the old sound is set after the new notes have been handed over. Both are set one output buffer ahead (5 to 12 ms): a fade set at the present moment can already be over when the audio thread gets to it, which would be a hard cut, and a message to an engine needs that long. If handing over took longer than planned, the old sound stays until the new one is there.
- **Picking up is one function: `chsNote`**, called from `schedule()` for every note that began before `from` and has not ended, when `play.chs` is set: after a clean switch, and whenever `schedule` is called with `first` (Play, which is also every jump, and the start of every loop round). Which engine does what:
  - Made of browser nodes (Sampler plain, Growl, Grid, SFX, Perc, drum kit and drum instruments, and the basic synths, Plant and Wavetable when the voice engine is off): the note is built with its real start time inside `pastShim` (it existed for held notes since the preset feature), so envelopes are where they would be and samples continue at the right spot. A 4 ms fade-in.
  - `al-poly` (basic synths, Plant, Acid, Wavetable): the note starts now with the length that is left, and the engine gets the age of the note (`q[8]`): amplitude envelope, filter envelope, FM envelope, the LFOs and the wavetable position start where they would be by now; the level is reached in 4 ms. The oscillators start fresh.
  - `al-grain` (Sampler in grain mode): the same with `q[6]`, and the scan through the sample carries on from where it would be.
  - Emit, Vector Grain, Vector FM, Orbit, Venom: the cheap way, the note starts now with the length that is left and its own attack.
- **Effect presets**: `chsFxWatch` (in the same wrapper of `changed()`, before the rest of the change is worked through) compares `f.p.pre` of every effect with what it was; the kinds with memory are in `CHSMEM`. Master effects are covered too.
- **No cost while nothing changes**: there is no timer and no per-note bookkeeping. `schedule()` has two extra comparisons per note. The v0.183 note list (every note pushed into a list while playing) is gone.
- Direct edits outside the new block: `schedule()` (one track only, the chase hook, the loop end), the two worklets (the age of a note), `pvPlay` and `gvPlay` (the age travels with the note), `pastShim` (a source that would have ended already must not throw), `stop()` (the sweep fix), `chgSwap`.

### What it does
- **A pin button** in the title row of every instrument, effect, MIDI effect and analyser (also the particle devices and the master chain), just left of the remove ✕ (instruments have no ✕: at the right end). Tooltip: "Pin: show this device in its own window over the arrangement, so you can watch and change it while you work on other tracks". It stays visible when the device is slid shut.
- **The window**: the device itself, on a solid dark body (no glass, so a moving picture never makes glass recalculate), a thin border and a shadow, not resizable. On top a slim tab in the mixer's style: dotted grip, device name, a coloured dot with the track name, and a lit pin that unpins. Drag the tab to move it; it stays inside the screen (also when the window is made smaller). A click anywhere in a window brings it to the front. Several windows at once, from one track or from many.
- **Where a new window goes**: above the device, over the arrangement (not over the device panel when there is room), moving down and right until it covers no more than a quarter of a window that is already there; on a full screen the place with the least overlap.
- **In the device panel** a pinned device leaves a slim placeholder (132 px, dashed, striped): its name with a lit pin (click: unpin), and "Pinned. Shown in its own window. Click to bring it to the front." Decision: a placeholder rather than the full device, so every control exists in exactly one place and nothing is drawn twice.
- **It is the same device**: every knob, picture, preset menu, Change, on/off, the arrow and the remove ✕ work in the window. A value changed somewhere else (automation, a macro, a modulator, the bridge) moves the knob in the window too (checked every 120 ms; only changed controls are painted).
- **Pictures keep running** while another track is selected: a spectrum or meter of the drums can be watched while working on the lead.
- **Kept with the project** (`S.pins`: track, device, place, height), so they come back after a reload and with the project file. Not in the undo history: undo and redo keep the open windows. A pinned device whose track or device is removed closes its window; undo of that removal brings the window back. When Change replaces a pinned effect in its place, the window follows the new effect.

### How it works (for the next person)
- The look of the panel is written for `#fxin` (345 rules). At start `pinCss()` rewrites those selectors to `:is(#fxin,.pinb)`, which keeps their weight, so a window body (`.pinb`) gets the exact panel look.
- A pinned device is rendered by the panel itself. `renderFx` is wrapped: for every track with a pinned device whose window needs a new copy (no copy yet, the device object changed, or an event in its window), the panel is rendered once for that track with `selT` swapped (`PIN.inner`), the pinned devices are taken out (`pinHarvest`) and moved into their windows, then the panel is rendered for the selected track as usual and its pinned devices are replaced by placeholders. Their controls get their own ids (`pn:<track>/<device>:fx:...`) so the next panel render does not delete them. Selecting a track renders only the panel (measured: median 31 ms with three pins on other tracks against 32 to 36 ms without pins; pin or unpin 33 to 39 ms).
- The panel's event handlers are recorded when they are added to `#fxin` (`FXH`, a line in the existing `fxin.addEventListener` wrapper) and also listen on every window (`pinMirror`; the panel's drag-to-scroll is left out). An early capture listener on the window (`PINCAP`, registered before all others) makes an event inside a window run with that window's track as the selected track and its pictures as the picture lists; it is put back after the event (the last window listener, a timer, and the start of every frame). `changed` and `renderFx` step out of the swap while they run.
- Pictures: `drawIV` is wrapped; once per picture frame (`FRN`) the windows are drawn with their own track and lists (`pinDraw`). The panel's lists no longer hold the canvases of its pinned devices, so nothing is drawn twice.
- Pointing at a picture in a window wakes the pictures like a picture in the panel does.

### Research (two research passes on the web, about 60 pages read; what is well supported, what is one source, what is a guess is said per line)

**How a sub should sound**
- Range: the sub band is 20 to 60 or 80 Hz; synth bass notes sit at 40 to 80 Hz (MusicRadar "ultimate guide to sub bass", Native Instruments blog, EDMProd; well supported). Best keys F to A, 43.7 to 55 Hz (EDMProd, one source); "centre on low G (49 Hz), do not go below E flat (39 Hz)" (Computer Music, one source). Both agree. The Key level control and the default picture are built around 49 Hz for that reason.
- Club systems: ordinary subs are usable to the mid 40s Hz, 40 Hz is common, 30 Hz is rare (ProSoundWeb "25 Hz is the new 50 Hz", one live engineer's measurements). No source gives a standard cutoff. The picture marks 30, 40 and 60 Hz for that reason.
- Per genre (thin evidence, said so by the sources): trance and techno most energy at 50 to 60 Hz, dubstep mid to high 40s (ProSoundWeb); dubstep sub in the 30 to 60 Hz octave (Sound On Sound "Dubstep basics"); 808 notes 32 to 49 Hz; drum and bass around E; psytrance bass between E and A with less than an octave of usable range (Psynews forum: folklore, but it matches the above). Nothing reliable found for house.
- Wave: sine first; triangle or light saturation for small speakers (MusicRadar, Native Instruments, EDMProd: well supported). Mike Senior (Sound On Sound) prefers triangle over saw or square for a sub layer and warns against detuned subs because the beating moves the level of the fundamental. So the instrument has one voice and no detune.
- Small speakers: they stop at 100 to 200 Hz, phones play little under 100 Hz (Aalto virtual bass paper DAFx 2020; Mastering The Mix). The ear rebuilds a missing low note from its overtones (Schouten's residue pitch; Licklider 1954; Wikipedia "Missing fundamental"). The Waves MaxxBass patent (US 5,930,373) asks for at least three overtones in a row above the speaker's cutoff: for notes of 40 to 60 Hz the 3rd to 5th, for 60 to 120 Hz the 2nd to 4th, anything higher at least 12 dB lower. Even overtones alone are heard an octave too high, so a full row is needed (Aalto paper; NTU thesis by Oo on the choice of nonlinear device). **No source gives a simple "2nd and 3rd at X dB" rule.** The level of the "Phones" knob (100% = the strongest added overtone as loud as the note, falling 9 dB per octave) is my choice, guided by the 3 to 10 dB per octave weighting in the Aalto paper.
- Mono: below about 100 Hz (MusicRadar), 80 to 100 Hz, sometimes 150 or 200 Hz (folklore). Reasons: clubs and small speakers sum to mono, phase cancellation, vinyl (the vinyl rule is disputed by a Masterdisk engineer). The instrument is mono, and its optional Width only touches overtones above 150 Hz.
- Phase: a click is a fast jump in level; a wave that starts at a random point with no fade-in can start at its top (KVR thread quoting a Waldorf FAQ, one source). Free-running oscillators give every note another level and punch (forum). Ableton Operator has a retrigger switch that "restarts the waveform at the same position in its phase" (Ableton manual). So: Same start, on by default, and a Start phase knob.
- Fade times: no reputable source gives milliseconds, only "raise the attack until the click goes". A rule of thumb from the KVR thread: a change over t ms spreads over about 1000/t Hz. Release 50 to 100 ms (Beat Kitchen, one source). The defaults (5 ms in, 60 ms out) are my choice from those.
- 808: pitch drop 1 to 2 semitones in 10 to 30 ms or 2 to 4 in 20 to 50 ms (Audeobox, one source); 44 ms pitch decay with 1 s amp decay (MusicRadar); the real TR-808 "sighs" from about 58 to 48 Hz over 300 ms and has a 6 ms transient at the start (Werner, DAFx 2014; Sound On Sound "Practical bass drum synthesis"). Decay 1 to 5 s by tempo, release 100 to 250 ms, soft clip, low pass at 500 to 800 Hz (Audeobox, EDMProd; single sources). Glide times: nothing found, my choice.
- Loudness against the key (ISO 226:2003, worked out from the standard's parameters as in the University of Surrey toolbox): a 40 Hz tone needs 14 dB more level than an 80 Hz tone to sound as loud at 40 phon, 12 dB at 60, 10 dB at 80, 9 dB at 90. So about 10 dB per octave at club level. "Key level" at 100% is that slope, limited to 12 dB either way.
- Levels and ducking: **no reputable figures** for sub against kick in dB, or for ducking depth per genre. What there is: up to 6 dB of ducking before it is heard as pumping, attack as fast as possible (Attack Magazine); 808 peaks at -6 to -3 dB (one source). The device's level is set by measurement against the Fractals kick instead (see tests).

**Algorithms**
- A sum of exact sine overtones has no aliasing at all as long as every overtone stays under half the sample rate; PolyBLEP and its relatives only reduce it (Välimäki, Pekonen, Nam, JASA 2012: two-point PolyBLEP is clean by ear up to about 2.1 kHz, no absolute figures). For a sub the sum is cheap (a saw at 50 Hz has 441 overtones under Nyquist, but nobody needs more than a few dozen in a sub). Chosen: a sum of up to 96 overtones.
- Drive without aliasing: oversampling and antiderivative anti-aliasing (Parker, Zavalishin, Le Bivic DAFx 2016; Chowdhury's CCRMA notebook) both only reduce it. Because the wave here is periodic and known, the driven wave is worked out for one cycle and turned back into overtone levels (a Fourier series), which is exact. This is the idea behind Chebyshev waveshaping ("additive synthesis with one oscillator", Csound manual; Puckette's book, which notes it is only exact at full level, so the drive sits before the envelope here). That this replaces oversampling is my own conclusion; it is confirmed by the measurements below.
- DC: the usual DC blocker with R = 0.995 cuts at about 35 Hz at 44.1 kHz, inside the sub band (worked out from J. O. Smith's formula). Here there is no DC to remove: the constant part of the Fourier series is left out. Low cut is an ordinary two-pole filter (minimum phase: linear phase rings before the note, Mastering The Mix and Craig Anderton), off by default.
- Known instruments: SubLab (one oscillator, sampler and "X-Sub", a sub held between 30 and 65 Hz plus a row of overtones with odd and even balance, that skips filter and effects; MusicRadar review; Future Audio Workshop's blog describes shifting the phases of the overtones to lower the peaks). Serum's sub oscillator with "direct out" past filter and effects. MaxxBass and Renaissance Bass (split at 60 to 110 Hz, make overtones of what is below, mix with or replace the original; Waves manuals). Submarine, bx_subsynth and dbx 120 make lower octaves instead. From these: the overtones for small speakers are added after the drive and do not touch the sub, and their phases are spread (Schroeder phases) so they raise the peak as little as possible.

**Presets**: sine with short fades for the club; triangle or saturation for small speakers (tube drive 60%, mix 40%: Native Instruments); techno rumble on F with slow attack, low pass and overdrive (Attack Magazine); drum and bass sine sub around E under a reese; dubstep two sines an octave apart (Sound On Sound); 808 as above; psytrance one saw, low pass a third open, no attack, short decay, low sustain, no release, 16th notes that skip the kick (one blog and the Psynews forum; a sine layer under it is disputed among producers).

### Built (names `sbs` / `SBS`, code before `askChoice`; `sbsInit();` sits right after `orbInit();`)
- **Sub** (`PRESETS.sbass`, first in "Bass & acid" in the instrument browser, with its picture; the old basic "Sub bass" stays). Worklet `al-sbs` (`SBSWL`, added to both module lists in `wlLoad`; `pvAny`, the engine list of the clean preset switch `CHSENG` and the list inside `chsCut` cover it).
- **The sound engine**: one voice at a time, a sum of up to 96 exact overtones worked out with one sine and one cosine per sample. `sbsCore()` is shared by the page and the worklet:
  - `stat()`: the fixed part of the wave as overtone levels. Shape (sine towards a triangle, square or saw, each built from its overtones with a soft roll-off at the top), 2nd and 3rd, then Drive (a soft ceiling, tanh; Asymmetry moves the wave off centre before it, which adds even overtones) worked out on one cycle and turned back into overtones, then the Octave up layer. The note itself always keeps the same level and phase, whatever is added. Worked out on the page (1 to 2 ms with drive, kept in a cache) and sent to the engine.
  - `dyn()`: per note and 3000 times a second: adds the overtones for small speakers (those between about 100 Hz and the 10th overtone, strongest just above 125 Hz, falling 9 dB per octave, spread in phase) and fades out any overtone that comes near 47% of the sample rate, also while the pitch drops or glides.
  - `keyLv()`: Key level.
  - No DC by construction. Nothing is oversampled and nothing is clipped; there is no limiter inside.
- **Notes**: Same start (every note starts at Start phase; off: a note carries on where the wave was). A new note fades the one before over the time of its own fade-in, so two notes never add up to more than one. Glide: Off, Legato (a note that starts while another is held slides there, the wave runs on unbroken, the envelope does not start again; when the later note ends first, the pitch slides back to the one still held), Always. Pitch drop (amount and time). Octave shift.
- **Envelope**: fade-in as an S curve, decay and release with no corner at their start, and the whole envelope runs through a 0.4 ms smoother, so no setting can make a step.
- **After the voice**: Tone (two-pole low pass) and Low cut (two-pole high pass), both skipped entirely at the ends of their range; Width (overtones above 150 Hz turned in opposite directions left and right; the note and everything under 150 Hz stay in the middle).
- **Duck to kick**: a button. The app has no sidechain that listens to another track's sound in an export: the Sidechain effect is a pump in time with the song whose `f.p.src` only marks the source track (for the cable and so the source keeps playing), and the Sidechain follower modulator reads a level meter once per screen frame and does nothing in an export. So the button adds the Sidechain effect after the device with the kick track marked as its source (it looks for a Kick designer track, then a track called kick, then a drum kit), and takes it away again. One undo step each.
- **Picture** (`sbsDraw`, `sbsPaint`): one cycle of the wave on top with the start point and the peak level in dB (red when it would clip); below it the note and every overtone as bars against note names (top) and Hz (bottom), with dashed lines at 30, 40 and 60 Hz and a red hatched area "Small speakers cannot play this" up to 120 Hz. The bars include the two filters. While a note plays the picture shows that key and glows. It is painted once into a spare canvas and only copied until a setting, the key or the size changes (0 paints per second at rest, measured). A click plays the last note.

### Not verified, and what is a guess
- **Nobody has listened to it.** Everything was set by measurement and from the research. The presets need the user's ears, most of all the levels of the Phones overtones, the drive amounts and the 808 times.
- The level of the small-speaker overtones, the default fade times, the glide times and the 10 ms class of note-to-note fades are my choices; the sources give no numbers for them.
- Drive sits before the envelope, so it does not get cleaner as a note dies away (an 808 keeps its colour to the end). A real saturator would. This is the price of having no aliasing.
- While the pitch drops fast, the overtone levels are updated 3000 times a second and glide in between. Not measured as a spectrum (a sweep has no steady overtones); the step test shows no jumps.
- Real mouse or trackpad, the Mac build, MIDI learn and automation of the knobs, modulators on the knobs.
- Tone and Low cut jump over a very small step when they are turned to the very end of their range while a note sounds (they switch off there).
