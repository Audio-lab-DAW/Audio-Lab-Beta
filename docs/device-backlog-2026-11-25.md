## Task A: Orbit synth (a new instrument)
> "Let's also make a device also based on physics. So you have a center mass that you can change the size of the mass, and then you can drop in small little balls that orbit around the center mass. You can change their mass, make them orbit faster or slower, add more or less. And as they orbit around the center mass, it creates sound on the synthesizer. And then on the right, I want to have some extra parameters. And yeah, you can figure out how a synthesizer would work like that. And add all the relevant features that you think are necessary with the right buttons and sliders."

Reading: an instrument in the particle look (black picture on the left, controls floating on the right). A centre mass whose size is a knob and can be dragged in the picture. Click in the picture to drop a ball; it gets the speed of a circular orbit at that distance (drag while dropping to throw it instead). Per ball: mass. Global: number of balls, orbit speed, gravity. Real gravity (inverse square, softened near the centre), a stable step method (symplectic, for example velocity Verlet or leapfrog), seeded, inside the worklet. How it makes sound is the designer's choice; a sensible design: every ball is an oscillator voice of the held note whose pitch interval, level, pan and filter follow its orbit (distance from the centre = pitch or brightness, angle = pan, speed = level or FM amount), with a choice of what each orbit value drives, as in the Vector devices. ADSR, presets (at least eight), one picture for all of it.

## Task B: Bouncing ball devices (instrument, effect, MIDI generator) with real physics tied to the rhythm
> "And Ableton has a device where there is like a bouncing ball. I think it's some sort of delay. Let's have a bouncing ball device as a instrument and also as an effect. And as the ball bounces, it makes sounds. And maybe also have like a MIDI trigger device. So as the ball bounces, it triggers MIDI, and then you can connect it to like, you can have like a MIDI melody generator based on the bouncing balls in the device. And also do like some harmonies and chords also. And then you can pick like a rhythm and yeah. But I want the physics to be actual physics and to be tied to the rhythm. So if you pick like a rhythm 1/4 or every bar, it should actually bounce every bar, but also be real physics and match it up. So make it work. Do as much research online on how physics like these work, how to do it as efficiently as possible so it doesn't suck up all the CPU. And do some research on how other VSTs do it, do some research on how simple games do physics like this, and how you can sync it up with actual rhythm while having it at the same time also be actual simulated physics algorithm."

What exists: Bouncy Notes (v0.166, walls in v0.177) is a MIDI effect where every note drops a ball and every bounce plays the note again (`bnBalls`, `bnRun`, `bnDraw`, closed-form bounce times). It is not tied to the tempo.
Reading:
1. Rhythm sync that is still real physics: the time between two bounces of a ball is 2·v/g, and every bounce multiplies v by the restitution e. So for a chosen note length T the gravity (or the drop height) is solved so that the first interval is exactly T; with e = 1 the ball bounces every T for ever, with e = 1/2 the intervals are T, T/2, T/4 (a ratchet), with e = 2/3 a triplet feel, and so on. Offer the rhythm choice (1/16 to 1 bar and longer), a "Bounce" choice (steady, halving, and free friction), and say in the log what was solved for. Research first (briefly): Ableton's own bouncing devices (Bouncy Notes in Inspired by Nature; look for a bouncing-ball delay), other plug-ins with physics sequencing, how simple games do ball physics (fixed time step, semi-implicit Euler, analytic solutions between collisions), and write the sources into the log.
2. A MIDI generator ("Bounce Melody" or a mode of Bouncy Notes): balls bounce on a keyboard or on a row of pads in the picture; each bounce plays a note from a chosen scale; options for harmonies (add a third, a fifth, an octave in the scale) and chords (each bounce plays a chord of the scale, with a chord progression per bar that the user can set); rhythm per ball. It must drive the track's instrument like the other MIDI effects (`MFXRUN`), work in exports, and be deterministic.
3. An instrument: the bounces excite a simple sound of their own (struck resonator or pluck, pitch from the key that was played, loudness from the impact speed).
4. An audio effect: a bouncing-ball delay: the repeats of the sound come at the bounce times of a ball (getting closer together and softer, or steady when synced), with pan following the ball.
All of them: the calculation of bounce times is closed form (no simulation steps in the audio thread), which is the efficient way.

## Task D: Change button with live browsing
> "And then to the right of the preset button, you have like a change button. So you can press that and then you go back into instruments or effects. And I want those effects to change live. If the project is playing or paused, it doesn't matter. So you press change, then you go into the window for the browsers, instrument browser, audio browser. Reading: Change opens the instrument browser (for an instrument) or the effect browser (for an effect) in "change" mode. One click on an entry swaps the device on the track at once, so it is heard right away whether the song plays or not (playing notes carry on with the new sound; when stopped, a click also plays a short test note). Another click swaps again. A double-click anywhere on an entry (or Enter) keeps it and closes the browser; Escape or closing puts the original device back with its settings. One undo step for the whole visit. The instrument browser already has a live mode (`IBCTX`, `ibLiveRender`, `openBrowser`); build on it.

## Task E: CPU: light devices and freezing heavy ones in the background
> "I want all these devices to be optimized for CPU use, so everything needs to stay snappy. And for any like heavy physics or any other heavy device, we need a way to dynamically freeze it in the background so it doesn't, so it's not taking up CPU all the time, unless there is too many changing parameters. Maybe we can figure out an algorithm or a way to do things like that."

Reading: (1) measure every particle device and the other heavy instruments (audio thread load per device, the app has `cpuLoad` and render capacity read-outs) and remove waste (pictures that redraw while nothing moves, engines that run with no notes). (2) Automatic background freeze: when a track's notes, instrument and effect settings have not changed for a while and the track is not selected for playing by hand, render it in the background (the app has freeze: `tools/engine-tests/freeze_match.py`, the Freeze button of the track controls) and play the rendered audio instead of the live devices; any change to the track (a knob, a note, a modulator or automation that reaches it) thaws it at once and seamlessly. A setting to switch it off, a small mark on the track that shows it is frozen in the background, and never a change in sound (compare frozen and live renders in a test). Tracks with modulators or automation on their devices stay live ("too many changing parameters").
